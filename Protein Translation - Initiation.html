Introduction to Initiation

Translation initiation is the highly regulated first stage of protein synthesis. It is the rate-limiting step in translation and a major target for cellular control mechanisms.

The primary goal of this stage is to assemble the ribosome onto the mRNA and position the initiator tRNA exactly at the start codon (AUG). This ensures the reading frame is set correctly for the synthesis of the polypeptide chain.

Key Components Required:

  • Small and Large Ribosomal Subunits (30S/50S or 40S/60S)
  • Messenger RNA (mRNA)
  • Initiator tRNA (charged with Methionine or N-formylmethionine)
  • Initiation Factors (IFs or eIFs)
  • Energy source (GTP and ATP)

Prokaryotic Initiation Mechanism

1. Formation of the 30S Initiation Complex

The 30S small ribosomal subunit binds to two initiation factors, IF1 and IF3.

  • IF1: Binds to the A-site, blocking tRNA binding there and ensuring initiator tRNA enters the P-site.
  • IF3: Binds to the E-site and prevents the 50S subunit from associating prematurely. It is crucial for fidelity.

2. mRNA Binding & Shine-Dalgarno

The 30S subunit binds to the mRNA. Precise positioning is achieved via base pairing between the Shine-Dalgarno (SD) sequence (consensus 5'-AGGAGG-3') on the mRNA and the complementary anti-SD sequence at the 3' end of the 16S rRNA.

This aligns the AUG start codon specifically at the P-site.

3. Recruitment of fMet-tRNA

IF2, complexed with GTP, recruits the fMet-tRNAfMet (initiator tRNA) to the P-site. The anticodon of the tRNA base pairs with the AUG start codon.

4. Formation of 70S Complex

Conformational changes trigger the hydrolysis of GTP bound to IF2. IF1, IF2-GDP, and IF3 are released. The 50S large subunit joins the 30S complex to form the functional 70S Initiation Complex. The ribosome is now ready for elongation.

Initiator tRNA & Formyl-Methionine

Initiator tRNA (tRNAfMet)

Distinct from elongator tRNA (tRNAmMet). It has a unique structure (e.g., unmatched base pair in acceptor stem) recognized by IF2, allowing it to enter the P-site directly.

Elongator tRNA (tRNAmMet)

Used for internal methionines. It binds only to the A-site complexed with EF-Tu and cannot be formylated.

Synthesis of N-Formylmethionine

Step 1: Charging
Methionine is attached to tRNAfMet by Met-tRNA synthetase.
Met + tRNA + ATP → Met-tRNA + AMP + PPi

Step 2: Formylation
A specific enzyme, Methionyl-tRNA transformylase, adds a formyl group to the amino group of methionine using N10-formyl-tetrahydrofolate as a donor.
Met-tRNAfMet + N10-formyl-THF → fMet-tRNAfMet + THF

Significance of Formylation

  • Peptide Bond Mimicry: The blocked N-terminus (formyl group) resembles a peptide bond, facilitating entry into the P-site.
  • Directionality: It prevents the N-terminus from reacting, ensuring polymerization only occurs at the C-terminus (N to C synthesis).
  • Recognition: The formyl group is a key recognition element for IF2 binding.

Eukaryotic Initiation Mechanism

Much more complex than prokaryotes, involving over 12 eukaryotic Initiation Factors (eIFs).

1. 43S Pre-initiation Complex Assembly

The 40S subunit binds eIF1, eIF1A, and eIF3. Meanwhile, the Ternary Complex forms (Met-tRNAiMet + eIF2 + GTP). The Ternary Complex binds to the 40S subunit to form the 43S complex.

2. mRNA Activation

The mRNA is prepared by the eIF4F complex (Cap-binding complex):

  • eIF4E: Binds 5' Cap
  • eIF4G: Scaffold protein
  • eIF4A: Helicase (unwinds secondary structure)

3. Scanning Model

The 43S complex binds to the 5' end of the mRNA. Driven by ATP hydrolysis (eIF4A helicase activity), it scans downstream (5' → 3') until it encounters the start codon (AUG) embedded in the Kozak Sequence (CRCCAUGG).

4. 60S Subunit Joining

Upon AUG recognition, eIF2 hydrolyzes GTP (aided by eIF5), releasing eIFs. eIF5B-GTP mediates the joining of the 60S large subunit. GTP hydrolysis on eIF5B releases the remaining factors, forming the 80S Initiation Complex.
