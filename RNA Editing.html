RNA Editing

Beyond the Central Dogma in Eukaryotes

Definition

A post-transcriptional molecular process in which the nucleotide sequence of an RNA molecule is altered via insertion, deletion, or conversion of nucleotides, resulting in an mRNA sequence that differs from the DNA template.

1. General Features & Scope

Where does it occur?

While initially discovered in the mitochondria of trypanosomes (kinetoplastids), RNA editing is widespread across eukaryotes. It occurs in the nucleus (mammals), mitochondria (trypanosomes, plants), and plastids (chloroplasts).

Timing

Occurs post-transcriptionally.

Specificity

Highly specific; enzymes recognize specific secondary structures or sequence motifs.

Types of Editing

  • ▸ Substitution Editing: Chemical modification of a base (e.g., A→I, C→U). Common in mammals and higher plants.
  • ▸ Insertion/Deletion Editing: Adding or removing Uracils. Prominent in protozoan mitochondria (Trypanosomes).

2. Substitution Editing

A-to-I Editing (Adenosine to Inosine)

The most prevalent form in mammals. Mediated by the ADAR (Adenosine Deaminase Acting on RNA) family of enzymes.

Reaction: Adenosine + H₂O → Inosine + NH₃

Impact: The translation machinery reads Inosine (I) as Guanosine (G). Thus, an A is functionally converted to a G, potentially altering the amino acid sequence (Recoding).

C-to-U Editing (Cytidine to Uridine)

Involves deamination of Cytidine. Mediated by the APOBEC complex (CDAR - Cytidine Deaminase Acting on RNA).

Reaction: Cytidine + H₂O → Uridine + NH₃

Impact: Often creates stop codons or changes amino acid identity. Crucial in plant mitochondria and chloroplasts, as well as specific mammalian transcripts.

Example: Apolipoprotein B

The classic textbook example of tissue-specific C-to-U editing in mammals.

In The Liver

Unedited

The transcript contains the codon CAA (Glutamine) at position 2153.

Result: ApoB-100 (Full length protein)

Function: ApoB100 is the major apolipoprotein component of VLDL (Very Low-Density Lipoprotein) and LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein), responsible for transporting lipids from the liver to peripheral tissues.

In The Intestine

Edited

Enzyme ACF/APOBEC-1 deaminates C to U.
Sequence changes: CAA → UAA

⚠ UAA is a STOP codon.

Result: ApoB-48 (Truncated protein)

Function: ApoB48 is essential for chylomicron assembly and dietary lipid absorption in the small intestine.

3. Pan-Editing (Indels)

Found primarily in the kinetoplast mitochondria of Trypanosomes (e.g., Trypanosoma brucei). This mechanism can rewrite huge sections of mRNA, creating start codons, stop codons, and reading frames where none existed in the DNA.

Key Player: Guide RNA (gRNA)

Small RNAs encoded by minicircles in the kinetoplast DNA. They contain the complementary sequence to the edited product.

The "Editosome" Mechanism:

  1. 1 Anchoring gRNA base-pairs with the pre-mRNA just downstream of the editing site.
  2. 2 Cleavage Endonuclease cleaves the mRNA at the mismatch site.
  3. 3 Addition/Deletion TUTase adds Uracils (Insertion) or Exonuclease removes them (Deletion) to match the gRNA template.
  4. 4 Ligation RNA Ligase seals the backbone.

4. Biological Significance

🧬

Protein Diversity

Allows a single gene to encode multiple protein variants with distinct functions (e.g., ApoB, Serotonin receptors, Glutamate receptors). This increases the proteome complexity without increasing genome size.

🧠

Neurological Function

A-to-I editing is highly active in the brain. Editing of the Gria2 transcript (Glutamate receptor) is essential for preventing calcium permeability. Lack of editing leads to epilepsy and neuronal death.

🛡️

Defense Mechanism

ADAR enzymes can target double-stranded RNA produced by viruses, mutating their genetic code to suppress replication (Hypermutation). It also suppresses innate immune responses to self-dsRNA.

🐛

Evolutionary Repair

In mitochondria and chloroplasts, editing often corrects genomic mutations, restoring functional Start/Stop codons and conserving essential amino acids required for respiration/photosynthesis.
