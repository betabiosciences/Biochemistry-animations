Ramachandran Plot

What is a Ramachandran Plot?

The Ramachandran plot, developed by G.N. Ramachandran, is a fundamental tool in structural biology. It's a specific type of scatter plot used to visualize the energetically allowed and disallowed regions for the backbone dihedral angles, phi (φ) and psi (ψ), of amino acid residues in a protein structure. By analyzing these angles, scientists can assess the quality of a protein model and understand its secondary structure.

The plot's foundation is the hard-sphere model. This model treats atoms as solid spheres with defined van der Waals radii. The "allowed" regions in the plot correspond to φ/ψ angle combinations where atoms do not clash or overlap, while "forbidden" regions represent combinations that would cause steric hindrance, which is energetically unfavorable.

Allowed vs. Forbidden Regions

The Ramachandran plot is typically divided into three main areas based on steric feasibility:

  • Core (Most Favored) Regions: These are the vibrant purple areas on the plot. They represent φ/ψ combinations with no steric clashes, corresponding to the most stable and common secondary structures.
  • Allowed (Generously Allowed) Regions: The bright blue areas. Here, atoms are slightly closer than the sum of their van der Waals radii, creating minor steric strain. These conformations are less common but still possible.
  • Disallowed Regions: The dark, uncolored areas. In these conformations, atoms are too close, leading to significant steric clashes. Amino acid residues (except Glycine) are very rarely found in these regions.

Exceptions: Glycine and Proline

Glycine

Glycine lacks a side chain (its R-group is just a hydrogen atom). This absence of a bulky side chain removes many steric constraints, giving it far greater conformational freedom. Its Ramachandran plot shows allowed regions that are symmetric about the origin (0°, 0°), including areas that are forbidden for all other amino acids.

Proline

Proline's side chain is unique because it loops back and bonds to its own backbone nitrogen atom, forming a rigid five-membered ring. This ring structure severely restricts rotation around the φ angle, locking it at approximately -65°. Consequently, its Ramachandran plot shows only a very narrow, vertically-oriented allowed region.

The Proteomic Ramachandran Plot

A Proteomic Ramachandran plot aggregates the (φ, ψ) angles from a massive database of high-resolution protein structures (e.g., from the Protein Data Bank). Instead of just showing theoretical allowed/disallowed regions, it displays the actual observed distribution of residues as a density map. These plots provide a more nuanced, data-driven view of protein conformations and are invaluable for validating new structures against the known universe of protein folds.

Applications of the Ramachandran Plot

  • Structure Validation: Its primary use is to check the quality of experimentally determined or computationally predicted protein structures.
  • Model Refinement: During the process of solving a protein structure, the plot helps scientists identify and correct regions with unlikely backbone conformations.
  • Understanding Protein Dynamics: By identifying residues in less common "allowed" regions, researchers can gain insights into flexible or functionally important parts of a protein.
  • Protein Design: When designing new proteins, the plot serves as a guide to ensure that the designed sequence can fold into a stable, sterically valid conformation.
