Protein Translation

The Termination Stage

1. Overview & Release Factors

Termination is the final phase of protein synthesis. Unlike initiation and elongation, which require specific tRNAs, termination relies on protein factors called Release Factors (RFs) to recognize stop codons in the A-site of the ribosome.

Class I Release Factors

Responsible for specific codon recognition and hydrolysis of the peptidyl-tRNA bond.

  • Function: They mimic the shape of tRNA (molecular mimicry).
  • Mechanism: They contain a universally conserved GGQ motif (Gly-Gly-Gln) that protrudes into the Peptidyl Transferase Center (PTC) to trigger hydrolysis.
  • Binding site: They bind A-site of the ribosome.

Class II Release Factors

These are GTPases (GTP-binding proteins) that do not recognize codons directly.

Key Role: Class II RFs stimulate the dissociation of Class I RFs from the ribosome after the polypeptide chain has been released.

2. Termination in Prokaryotes

Prokaryotes utilize a dual-key system for codon recognition involving two distinct Class I factors.

RF1
Recognizes
UAA UAG
RF2
Recognizes
UAA UGA

The Role of RF3

RF3 is the prokaryotic Class II factor. It binds to the ribosome in a GDP-bound state.

  1. RF1 or RF2 binds the A-site and releases the peptide.
  2. The presence of RF1/RF2 induces RF3-GDP to bind.
  3. Interaction with the ribosome acts as a GEF (Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor), causing RF3 to exchange GDP for GTP.
  4. Binding of GTP induces a conformational change in RF3 that displaces RF1/RF2.
  5. RF3 hydrolyzes GTP to GDP and dissociates.

3. Ribosome Recycling (Prokaryotes)

After termination, the ribosome remains as a Post-Termination Complex (Post-TC) containing the 70S ribosome, mRNA, and a deacylated tRNA in the P-site. This must be disassembled to be reused.

Critical Factor: RRF

Ribosome Recycling Factor

RRF is a protein that structurally mimics a tRNA (nearly identical L-shape). It binds to the A-site of the Post-TC.

The Recycling Mechanism:

  • EF-G (Elongation Factor G) binds to the ribosome, complexed with GTP.
  • EF-G catalyzes a "translocation-like" event, pushing RRF deeper into the ribosome.
  • This mechanical stress acts like a wedge, splitting the 70S ribosome into 50S and 30S subunits.
  • IF3 binds the 30S subunit to release the final tRNA and mRNA, preventing re-association with the 50S subunit.

4. Termination in Eukaryotes

Eukaryotic termination is mechanistically similar but structurally distinct, utilizing a simpler recognition system but more complex regulation.

eRF1: The Universal Recognizer

Unlike prokaryotes, eukaryotes use a single Class I factor, eRF1, which recognizes all three stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA).

eRF1 is a structural mimic of tRNA, with distinct domains representing the anticodon loop (domain 1), the acceptor stem (domain 2, containing the GGQ motif), and the T-stem (domain 3).

eRF3 & ATP Hydrolysis

  • eRF3: A GTPase that associates with eRF1 *before* binding to the ribosome. GTP hydrolysis is required for eRF1 to undergo the conformational change necessary for peptide release.
  • Recycling Differences: Eukaryotes do not have a homolog to prokaryotic RRF. Instead, the ATPase ABCE1 (Rli1 in yeast) plays a central role in splitting the ribosomal subunits after termination.
