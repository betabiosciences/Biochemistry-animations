Chemiosmotic Theory & Chemiosmosis

The chemiosmotic theory, proposed by Peter Mitchell in 1961, explains how ATP is synthesized during oxidative phosphorylation.

Chemiosmosis refers to the process where energy stored in a proton gradient across a membrane is used to drive ATP synthesis.

Key concepts:

  • Electron transport chain pumps protons from the mitochondrial matrix to the intermembrane space
  • This creates an electrochemical proton gradient (proton motive force)
  • Protons flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase (Complex V)
  • This flow drives the rotation of ATP synthase and ATP synthesis

The proton gradient has two components:

  • pH gradient (chemical potential) - higher proton concentration in intermembrane space
  • Electrical potential - positive charge in intermembrane space, negative in matrix
