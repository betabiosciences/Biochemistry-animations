Types of RNAP III Promoters and General Transcription Factors

RNA Polymerase III (RNAP III) is responsible for transcribing small non-coding RNAs including tRNAs, 5S rRNA, U6 snRNA, and other small RNAs. Its promoters are unique in their positioning and transcription factor requirements.

Type 1 Promoters

Type 1 promoters are found in 5S rRNA genes and consist of internal control regions:

  • Intermediate Element (IE): Located around +50 to +60
  • A Block: Around +80 to +90
  • C Block: Around +70 to +80

Transcription Factors: TFIIIA, TFIIIB, and TFIIIC

TFIIIA binds specifically to the internal control region of 5S rRNA genes, followed by TFIIIC recruitment, which then positions TFIIIB upstream of the transcription start site.

Type 2 Promoters

Type 2 promoters are characteristic of tRNA genes and contain two highly conserved internal sequences:

  • A Block (TGGCNNAGTGG) at positions +8 to +19
  • B Block (GGTTCGANNCC) at positions +52 to +62

Transcription Factors: TFIIIB and TFIIIC

TFIIIC binds directly to the A and B blocks, serving as an assembly factor for TFIIIB, which then recruits RNAP III.

Type 3 Promoters

Type 3 promoters are found in genes such as U6 snRNA and 7SK RNA. Unlike types 1 and 2, these promoters are located upstream of the transcription start site and resemble RNAP II promoters.

  • Contain a TATA box around -30
  • Have a Proximal Sequence Element (PSE) around -60
  • May include a Distal Sequence Element (DSE) further upstream

Mechanism of Transcription

The transcription mechanism of RNAP III involves a precise sequence of events that begins with promoter recognition and culminates in transcription termination.

Transcription Initiation

The initiation process varies by promoter type but follows a general pattern:

  1. Promoter Recognition: Specific transcription factors recognize and bind to promoter elements
  2. Pre-initiation Complex Formation: Assembly of TFIIIB at the transcription start site
  3. RNAP III Recruitment: TFIIIB recruits RNAP III to the promoter
  4. DNA Melting: Local unwinding of DNA to form the transcription bubble

Key Insight: TFIIIB remains stably bound at the promoter, enabling multiple rounds of transcription without reassembly of the entire pre-initiation complex.

Transcription Termination

RNAP III termination is distinct from other RNA polymerases:

  • Termination occurs at a simple T-rich sequence in the DNA template
  • Typically, a run of 4-5 thymidine residues (T-tract) signals termination
  • No additional protein factors are required for termination
  • The nascent RNA forms a weak rU-dA hybrid that facilitates polymerase release

tRNA Processing and Export into Cytosol

Newly transcribed tRNA precursors undergo extensive processing before becoming functional molecules in the cytosol.

5' and 3' End Processing

tRNA transcripts contain extra sequences at both ends that must be removed:

  • 5' Leader Removal: Catalyzed by RNase P, a ribozyme with catalytic RNA and protein components
  • 3' Trailer Removal: Performed by endonucleases such as RNase Z that cleave after the discriminator base
  • CCA Addition: The sequence CCA is added to the 3' end by tRNA nucleotidyltransferase if not encoded in the gene

Intron Splicing

Some tRNA genes contain introns that must be removed:

  • tRNA introns are typically short (14-60 nucleotides) and located one nucleotide 3' to the anticodon
  • Splicing involves cleavage by tRNA splicing endonuclease followed by ligation by tRNA ligase
  • The process occurs in the nucleus before export to the cytoplasm

Nucleotide Modifications

tRNAs contain the highest density of post-transcriptional modifications of any RNA class:

  • Base Modifications: Methylation, deamination, reduction, and more complex alterations
  • Ribose Modifications: 2'-O-methylation and pseudouridylation
  • Over 100 different modifications have been identified in tRNAs
  • These modifications enhance stability, decoding accuracy, and folding

Nuclear Export

Processed tRNAs are exported from the nucleus to the cytoplasm through nuclear pore complexes:

  • Exportin-t (XPO-t) is the primary tRNA export receptor
  • Export requires RanGTP, which binds to Exportin-t and facilitates cargo release in the cytoplasm
  • Quality control mechanisms prevent improperly processed tRNAs from being exported.

Quality-control re-import/export cycles

The quality-control mechanisms that ensure only perfect tRNAs reach the cytoplasm.

  • Exportin-t essentially recognizes the tRNA’s 3D geometry rather than its sequence. Any misfolded structure stalls export.
  • Several modifications stabilize the L-shape or prevent misfolding. Unmodified or hypomodified tRNAs: (1) stay trapped in the nucleus (2) or get exported, then re-imported for correction (3) or get targeted for nuclear surveillance degradation.
  • Under nutrient stress or if misfolding is detected from the cytosol, the tRNA is sent back to the nucleus through a retrograde pathway (Importin-β family).
