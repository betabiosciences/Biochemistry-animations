Enzyme Kinetic Plots

Michaelis-Menten Plot Analysis

This plot shows the relationship between the initial reaction velocity (V₀) and the substrate concentration ([S]). The plot is defined by the Michaelis-Menten equation:

V₀ =
Vmax [S] Km + [S]
  • Vmax (Maximum Velocity): The rate of reaction when the enzyme is fully saturated with substrate. It is the plateau the curve approaches. On the graph, this is indicated by the horizontal dashed line.
  • Km (Michaelis Constant): The substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax (Vmax/2). A small Km indicates high affinity of the enzyme for its substrate, and vice versa.
  • Shape: The plot has a rectangular hyperbolic shape. At low [S], the velocity is directly proportional to [S]. At high [S], the velocity becomes independent of [S] and approaches Vmax.

Interact with the graph: Hover your mouse over the curve to see the precise V₀ and [S] values at any point. Use the sliders above to see how Vmax and Km affect the curve's shape.

Lineweaver-Burk (Double Reciprocal) Plot

This plot linearizes the Michaelis-Menten equation by plotting the reciprocal of velocity (1/V₀) against the reciprocal of substrate concentration (1/[S]).

1 V₀
=
Km Vmax
1 [S]
+
1 Vmax

This follows the form of a linear equation y = mx + c.

  • Y-intercept: The point where the line crosses the y-axis is equal to 1/Vmax.
  • X-intercept: The point where the line crosses the x-axis is equal to -1/Km.
  • Slope: The slope of the line is equal to Km/Vmax.

Advantage: It provides a more precise way to determine Vmax and Km from experimental data compared to the non-linear Michaelis-Menten plot.
Disadvantage: It gives undue weight to data points at low substrate concentrations (where 1/[S] is large), which are often the most susceptible to experimental error.

Eadie-Hofstee Plot Analysis

This is another linear transformation of the Michaelis-Menten equation, where V₀ is plotted against V₀/[S].

V₀ = -Km
V₀ [S]
+ Vmax
  • Y-intercept: The y-intercept gives a direct value of Vmax.
  • X-intercept: The x-intercept is equal to Vmax/Km.
  • Slope: The slope of the line is -Km.

Advantage: This plot does not compress the data points at low [S] as much as the Lineweaver-Burk plot.
Disadvantage: Both axes depend on V₀, so any experimental error in measuring velocity is present in both the x and y coordinates, complicating error analysis.

Hanes-Woolf Plot Analysis

In this plot, [S]/V₀ is plotted against [S]. This linearization is generally considered the most accurate for determining kinetic parameters.

[S] V₀
=
1 Vmax
[S] +
Km Vmax
  • Y-intercept: The y-intercept is equal to Km/Vmax.
  • X-intercept: The x-intercept is -Km.
  • Slope: The slope of the line is 1/Vmax.

Advantage: This plot provides a more even distribution of data points along the line compared to other linearizations, reducing the impact of errors at low substrate concentrations.
