ER Protein Quality Control: The Glycan Code

The endoplasmic reticulum employs a sophisticated surveillance system that monitors protein folding status through specific carbohydrate modifications, collectively termed the "glycan code."

The Calnexin/Calreticulin Cycle

1

Co-translational Modification

Newly synthesized proteins enter the ER with an N-linked glycan (Glc3Man9GlcNAc2) attached to Asn residues in the consensus sequence Asn-X-Ser/Thr.

↓
2

Glucosidase Trimming

Glucosidase I removes terminal glucose, followed by Glucosidase II removing the second glucose, creating the monoglucosylated GlcMan9GlcNAc2 structure.

↓
3

Lectin Binding

The monoglucosylated glycan is recognized by calnexin (membrane-bound) or calreticulin (soluble), which recruit PDI for disulfide bond formation.

↓
4

Glucose Release & Reglucosylation

Glucosidase II removes the final glucose. If misfolded, UGGT (UDP-glucose:glycoprotein glucosyltransferase) re-adds glucose, allowing another binding cycle.

ERAD Recognition: The Mannose Timer

Prolonged folding attempts expose the protein to ER mannosidase I and EDEMs (ER degradation-enhancing α-mannosidase-like proteins), which trim mannose residues to Man8, creating an degradation signal recognized by OS-9 lectins.

The ERAD Dislocation Complex

Membrane extraction: The ERAD complex forms a retrotranslocon channel. The p97 ATPase complex with cofactors, provides mechanical force for extracting proteins from the membrane. E3 ubiquitin ligases catalyze ubiquitination during extraction.

Ubiquitin Structure & Polyubiquitination Machinery

Ubiquitination is a post-translational modification that targets proteins for proteasomal degradation. The process involves a highly conserved 76-amino acid protein with remarkable structural versatility.

Ubiquitin Structure: The β-grasp Fold

Primary Sequence

Ubiquitin is a 76-residue polypeptide (8.6 kDa) with the C-terminal sequence -LRLRGG, where Gly76 forms an essential isopeptide bond with target Lys residues.

Three-dimensional Architecture

The structure features a β-grasp fold: five β-strands forming a mixed β-sheet (β1-β5), with an α-helix packed against one face. This creates a compact globular domain with a hydrophobic core and a distinct surface patch for protein-protein interactions.

Ubiquitin-binding Domains (UBDs)

Proteins contain specialized domains that recognize the hydrophobic patch or specific linkages, determining downstream fate.

The Three Enzyme Cascade

E1

Ubiquitin-activating enzyme (E1) forms a high-energy thioester bond with ubiquitin's C-terminus in an ATP-dependent reaction.

E2

Ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes (E2s) receive ubiquitin via transthiolation. ~40 E2s exist, each with specificity for different E3s and linkage types.

E3

Ubiquitin ligases (E3s) catalyze transfer to substrate Lys residues.

Polyubiquitin Linkage Specificity

K48-linkages: Form characteristic chains where each ubiquitin links via Lys48, creating a compact structure recognized by the proteasome. This is the canonical degradation signal.

K63-linkages: Typically non-degradative, involved in signaling and DNA repair. The linkage geometry differs, preventing proteasome recognition.

The 26S Proteasome: Structure & Degradation Mechanism

The 26S proteasome is a 2.5 MDa ATP-dependent protease that degrades ubiquitinated proteins. Its architecture provides sequence-unspecific but highly regulated proteolysis.

Overall Architecture

19S Regulatory Particle (RP)

The RP consists of base and lid subcomplexes. The base contains six AAA+ ATPases that form a heterohexameric ring, plus four non-ATPase subunits. The lid contains nine Rpn subunits including the deubiquitinating enzyme.

The 20S Core Particle (CP)

A barrel-shaped stack of four heptameric rings: α1-7 β1-7 β1-7 α1-7. The inner β-rings contain the catalytic subunits with caspase-like, trypsin-like, and chymotrypsin-like activities.

Gate mechanism: The α-subunit N-termini form a closed gate blocking substrate entry.

Substrate Processing Cascade

1

Recognition & Binding

Polyubiquitinated substrates bind proteasomal receptors via their Ubiquiting binding domain.

2

Deubiquitination

Dubs cleaves the entire ubiquitin chain as the substrate is engaged. This ATP-dependent process recycles ubiquitin and prevents chain shredding.

3

Translocation & Unfolding

The AAA+ motor pulls substrates through a <1.3 nm pore, applying high force. Unfolding occurs when mechanical force overcomes protein stability.

4

Proteolysis

Substrate enters the proteolytic chamber where three active sites cleave peptide bonds. Processive degradation releases 3-25 residue peptides. The average peptide length is 7-9 amino acids.

The Unfolded Protein Response: ER Stress Signaling

When ER protein folding capacity is exceeded, three integrated signaling pathways activate transcriptional and translational programs to restore proteostasis or initiate apoptosis.

IRE1α Pathway: The Most Conserved Arm

Sensing

Under non-stress conditions, BiP binds IRE1α's luminal domain, maintaining it as inactive monomers. Accumulating unfolded proteins bind BiP, freeing IRE1α to dimerize and trans-autophosphorylate its kinase domain.

Activation

Phosphorylation induces conformational changes activating the cytosolic RNase domain. IRE1α oligomerizes into higher-order assemblies at the ER membrane.

Execution

IRE1α excises a 26-nucleotide intron from XBP1u mRNA. This unconventional splicing creates a translational frameshift, generating XBP1s (spliced), a potent transcription factor.

XBP1s Targets

XBP1s upregulates ER chaperones (BiP, calreticulin), ERAD components and lipid synthesis enzymes. It also induces RIDD (Regulated IRE1-Dependent Decay), cleaving select mRNAs and miRNAs to reduce ER load.

PERK Pathway: Translational Control

Activation mechanism: Similar to IRE1α, PERK dimerizes and autophosphorylates when released from BiP. Its cytosolic kinase domain phosphorylates eIF2α at Ser51.

Consequences: eIF2α-P inhibits global translation initiation by sequestering eIF2B, reducing protein load. Paradoxically, it selectively enhances translation of mRNAs with upstream ORFs, including ATF4.

ATF4 function: This bZIP transcription factor induces expression of amino acid metabolism genes, antioxidants, and pro-apoptotic CHOP (C/EBP homologous protein).

ATF6 Pathway: Transcriptional Induction

Stress Sensing & Activation

ATF6α (90 kDa) is a type II transmembrane protein. Under stress, it translocates to the Golgi where proteases sequentially cleave it, releasing the cytosolic bZIP domain.

Nuclear Translocation

The cleaved ATF6α fragment (50 kDa) enters the nucleus, binding ERSE (ER stress response element) and UPRE (unfolded protein response element) promoters.

Integrated Stress Response & Cross-talk

Temporal dynamics: ATF6α responds first (peak 2-4h), followed by PERK (peak 4-8h), then IRE1α (sustained >8h). This sequential activation allows graded responses.

Apoptotic switch: Persistent stress activates CHOP, which upregulates BIM, BAX, and downregulates BCL-2.

ER-phagy: Severe stress activates ER-resident autophagy receptors that fragment ER tubules for lysosomal degradation, reducing ER volume and protein synthesis capacity.
