Signaling Components

Receptors

Proteins that bind specific ligands with high affinity and specificity. They undergo conformational changes upon binding, initiating signal transduction cascades.

Transducers

Molecules that convert one type of signal to another. They amplify the initial signal and often involve enzymatic activities like kinases or phosphatases.

Second Messengers

Small molecules like cAMP, IP3, or Ca²⁺ that relay signals from receptors to target proteins, amplifying the signal throughout the cell.

Adapters

Proteins that connect different components of signaling pathways without catalytic activity. They facilitate protein-protein interactions.

Bifurcators

Elements that split a single signal into multiple pathways, allowing one stimulus to produce diverse cellular responses simultaneously.

Integrators

Components that combine multiple signals to produce a unified cellular response, enabling complex decision-making processes in cells.

Scatchard Equation & Plot

The Scatchard plot is a graphical method for analyzing receptor-ligand binding data. It transforms the hyperbolic binding curve into a straight line, making it easier to determine the dissociation constant (Kd) and the maximum number of binding sites (Bmax).

Scatchard Equation:

B F
=
Bmax - B Kd


B = bound ligand, F = free ligand, Bmax = maximum binding, Kd = dissociation constant

Significance of Scatchard Plot

• Linear plot indicates single class of binding sites
• Slope = -1/Kd (binding affinity)
• X-intercept = Bmax (receptor density)
• Y-intercept = Bmax/Kd (binding capacity)
• Curved plots suggest multiple binding sites or cooperativity

Experimental Binding Curve Plotting

Binding curves are experimentally determined using radiolabeled ligands.

Total Binding: Measured by adding increasing amounts of radiolabeled ligand to the receptors. Both specific and non-specific binding are measured.

Non-specific Binding: Measured in the presence of a vast excess of unlabeled ligand, which saturates all specific binding sites, leaving only non-specific binding to be measured. This curve is linear.

Specific Binding: Calculated by subtracting the non-specific binding from the total binding. This is the true receptor-ligand binding curve.

Total Binding

Radiolabeled ligand + Receptors
Measures all bound ligand

Non-specific Binding

Radiolabeled ligand + Receptors + Excess unlabeled ligand
Measures non-specific interactions

Specific Binding

Total Binding - Non-specific Binding
True receptor-ligand interaction
