GPCR Signaling Pathway

G Protein-Coupled Receptors: Structure, Function, and Signaling Mechanisms

GPCR Basic Structure

G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) are a large family of cell surface receptors that detect molecules outside the cell and activate internal signal transduction pathways.

They share a common structure of seven transmembrane α-helices (7TM), an extracellular N-terminus, and an intracellular C-terminus.

The seven transmembrane domains form a pocket that binds ligands. The intracellular loops and C-terminal tail interact with G proteins.

GEF Activity of GPCRs

GPCRs act as Guanine nucleotide Exchange Factors (GEFs) for heterotrimeric G proteins.

When a ligand binds to the GPCR, it undergoes a conformational change that allows it to interact with and activate G proteins.

This interaction promotes the exchange of GDP for GTP on the Gα subunit, leading to dissociation of the Gα subunit from the Gβγ dimer.

Both Gα-GTP and Gβγ can then interact with various effector proteins to propagate the signal.

Heterotrimeric G-Protein Structure

Heterotrimeric G proteins are composed of three subunits: Gα, Gβ, and Gγ.

Gα

Binds GDP/GTP and has GTPase activity

Gβ

Forms tight complex with Gγ subunit

Gγ

Anchors Gβγ to membrane via lipid tail

G-Protein Types

There are four main classes of Gα subunits that define the signaling pathways:

Gs Type

Stimulates adenylyl cyclase, increasing cAMP levels. Activates PKA. Examples: β-adrenergic receptors.

Gi/o Type

Inhibits adenylyl cyclase, decreasing cAMP levels. Examples: α2-adrenergic receptors.

Gq Type

Activates phospholipase Cβ, generating IP3 and DAG. Increases intracellular calcium. Examples: α1-adrenergic receptors.

G12/13 Type

Activates RhoGEFs, regulating cytoskeletal changes and cell motility.
