Prokaryotic Transcription

Promoter & Transcription Factors

1. Overview of RNA Polymerases in Prokaryotes

In prokaryotes, transcription is carried out by a single type of RNA polymerase (RNAP) enzyme, unlike eukaryotes which have multiple RNA polymerases.

Key Features:

  • Prokaryotic RNA polymerase is a multi-subunit enzyme
  • It consists of a core enzyme and a sigma (σ) factor
  • The complete enzyme is called the holoenzyme: α₂ββ'ωσ
  • It synthesizes all types of RNA: mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA
  • No post-transcriptional modification is needed for mRNA in prokaryotes

RNA Polymerase Composition:

Core Enzyme: α₂ββ'ω (catalyzes RNA synthesis)

Sigma Factor: σ (recognizes promoter sequences)

Holoenzyme: α₂ββ'ωσ (complete, functional enzyme)

2. Features of Primary Transcripts

Primary transcripts in prokaryotes have distinct characteristics based on the type of RNA they will become.

mRNA Primary Transcript:

  • Often polycistronic (encodes multiple proteins)
  • No 5' cap structure
  • No poly-A tail at the 3' end
  • Transcription and translation are coupled
  • Short half-life (minutes)

rRNA Primary Transcript:

  • Contains sequences for 16S, 23S, and 5S rRNAs
  • Includes spacer regions that are removed during processing
  • Processed by RNase III and other nucleases
  • May also contain tRNA sequences in some operons

tRNA Primary Transcript:

  • May be transcribed as individual units or in clusters
  • Contains extra sequences at both 5' and 3' ends
  • Processed by RNase P (5' end) and RNase D (3' end)
  • Undergoes base modifications and addition of CCA at 3' end

3. Structure of the Core Promoter

The promoter is the DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to initiate transcription.

Core Promoter Elements:

Transcription Start Site (TSS): +1 position where transcription begins

-10 Box (Pribnow Box): TATAAT consensus sequence

-35 Box: TTGACA consensus sequence

Spacer Region: 16-18 bp between -10 and -35 boxes

Function of Each Element:

  • -35 Box: Initial recognition site for RNA polymerase holoenzyme
  • Spacer Region: Optimal spacing allows proper protein-DNA interactions
  • -10 Box: Site for DNA melting (open complex formation)
  • TSS: Position where first ribonucleotide is added

4. Additional Core Promoter Elements

Besides the core -10 and -35 elements, some promoters contain additional sequences that enhance transcription.

UP Element (Upstream Promoter Element):

  • Located upstream of the -35 box (around -40 to -60)
  • Rich in A-T base pairs
  • Interacts with α-subunit of RNA polymerase
  • Found in strong promoters like rRNA genes

Extended -10 Element:

  • Located immediately upstream of the -10 box
  • Consensus sequence: TGn
  • Compensates for weak -35 boxes
  • Directly contacts σ factor

Discriminator Region:

  • Located between -10 box and TSS
  • Sequence affects promoter strength
  • Important for stringent response regulation
  • Consensus: GGG or CCC in strong promoters

5. Structure of Bacterial RNA Polymerase Holoenzyme

The holoenzyme is the complete, functional form of RNA polymerase capable of transcription initiation.

Holoenzyme Composition: α₂ββ'ωσ

α-subunit (2 copies): Enzyme assembly, UP element recognition

β-subunit: Forms active center, RNA synthesis

β'-subunit: DNA binding, template strand interaction

ω-subunit: Enzyme assembly and stability, chaperone function

σ-factor: Promoter recognition, initiation specificity

Omega (ω) Subunit Details:

  • The omega subunit is the smallest component of RNA polymerase
  • Encoded by the rpoZ gene in E. coli
  • Plays a crucial role in enzyme assembly and stability
  • Acts as a chaperone that facilitates proper folding of the β' subunit
  • Helps protect RNA polymerase from denaturation under stress conditions
  • While not essential for basal transcription, it enhances enzyme efficiency and longevity
  • Interacts with the β' subunit and helps maintain its structural integrity

Holoenzyme Functions:

  • Promoter recognition and binding
  • DNA melting to form transcription bubble
  • Initiation of RNA synthesis
  • Abortive initiation cycling
  • Promoter clearance transition to elongation

6. Structure of Core Enzyme and Their Functions

The core enzyme (α₂ββ'ω) is responsible for the elongation phase of transcription after σ factor dissociation.

Core Enzyme Subunits:

α-subunit (αCTD and αNTD):
- αNTD: Dimerization, assembly platform
- αCTD: UP element recognition, activator interactions

β-subunit:
- Forms part of active center
- Binds nucleotides
- Catalyzes phosphodiester bond formation

β'-subunit:
- Contains Mg²⁺ ions at active site
- Interacts with template DNA strand
- Forms DNA-binding channel

ω-subunit:
- Stabilizes β'-subunit folding
- Enhances enzyme assembly and stability
- Provides protection against denaturation

Core Enzyme Functions:

  • RNA chain elongation
  • DNA unwinding and rewinding
  • Transcription fidelity maintenance
  • Response to transcription factors
  • Termination signal recognition

7. Structure of Sigma 70 Family Members

The σ⁷⁰ family is the primary group of sigma factors in E. coli, responsible for most transcription during normal growth.

Sigma 70 Domain Structure:

Region 1.1: Autoinhibitory, keeps σ inactive when free

Region 2: -10 box recognition, DNA melting

Region 3: Extended -10 recognition, spacer interaction

Region 4: -35 box recognition, activator contact

Types of σ⁷⁰ Family Members:

  • σ⁷⁰ (RpoD): Housekeeping genes, primary sigma factor
  • σ³² (RpoH): Heat shock response genes
  • σ²⁸ (RpoF): Flagellar and chemotaxis genes
  • σ³⁸ (RpoS): Stationary phase and stress response
  • σ²⁴ (RpoE): Extreme heat shock and envelope stress
  • σFecI: Iron transport genes

8. Promoter Melting by Sigma 2.4 Region

The sigma 2.4 region plays a critical role in promoter melting, the process of DNA strand separation that forms the transcription bubble.

Mechanism of Promoter Melting:

  • The σ factor region 2.4 specifically recognizes and binds to the -10 box sequence
  • This interaction induces structural changes in both DNA and RNA polymerase
  • Key aromatic residues in σ region 2.4 intercalate between DNA bases, destabilizing the double helix
  • The DNA melting begins at the -10 box and propagates downstream to the TSS
  • This creates the transcription bubble of approximately 12-14 base pairs

Key Steps in Promoter Melting:

1. Recognition: σ²·⁴ region binds to -10 box consensus sequence

2. Intercalation: Aromatic residues insert between DNA bases

3. Destabilization: Hydrogen bonds between strands break

4. Bubble Formation: 12-14 bp transcription bubble forms

5. Stabilization: β' subunit stabilizes the melted DNA region

Structural Features of σ Region 2.4:

  • Contains a helix-turn-helix motif for DNA binding
  • Has conserved aromatic residues (tryptophan, tyrosine) for intercalation
  • Positioned to interact with the non-template strand in the -10 region
  • Works in concert with region 2.3 which helps stabilize the melted DNA

Regulation of Sigma Factors by Anti-Sigma Factors

Anti-sigma factors are proteins that bind to specific sigma factors and inhibit their activity, providing an important regulatory mechanism.

Mechanisms of Anti-Sigma Factor Action:

Direct Binding: Anti-sigma factors bind σ factors, blocking RNAP interaction

Conformational Change: Binding induces structural changes that inhibit σ function

Sequestration: Anti-sigma factors prevent σ from accessing RNAP core enzyme

Promoter Blocking: Some anti-sigma factors interfere with promoter recognition

Well-Characterized Anti-Sigma Factors:

  • Rsd (Regulator of Sigma D): Binds to σ⁷⁰ during stationary phase
  • FlgM: Anti-σ²⁸ factor that regulates flagellar biosynthesis
  • RseA: Membrane-bound anti-σᴱ factor that responds to envelope stress
  • ChrR: Anti-σᴿ factor that responds to oxidative stress

9. Structure of Sigma 54 Family Members

σ⁵⁴ (RpoN) is structurally and functionally distinct from the σ⁷⁰ family and regulates nitrogen metabolism and other specialized functions.

Sigma 54 Unique Features:

Different promoter recognition: -24 (GG) and -12 (GC) elements

Requires activator proteins: Cannot initiate transcription alone

ATP-dependent activation: Uses bacterial enhancer-binding proteins

Closed complex formation: Does not spontaneously melt DNA

Genes Regulated by σ⁵⁴:

  • Nitrogen assimilation (glnA, ntr genes)
  • Hydrogen metabolism (hox genes)
  • Flagellar synthesis in some bacteria
  • Certain pilus and virulence genes
  • Various carbon source utilization genes

Structural Comparison with σ⁷⁰:

  • σ⁵⁴ lacks region 1.1 found in σ⁷⁰ factors
  • Has different domain organization for promoter recognition
  • Contains unique regions for activator interaction
  • Forms stable closed complexes that require activation
