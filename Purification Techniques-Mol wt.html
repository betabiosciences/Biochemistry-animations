Gel Filtration Chromatography

Gel filtration chromatography, also known as size exclusion chromatography (SEC), separates molecules based on their size and shape. The stationary phase consists of porous beads. Larger molecules cannot enter the pores and elute first, while smaller molecules enter the pores and take a longer, more tortuous path, eluting later.

Key points:

  • Does not rely on binding interactions between the protein and resin
  • Excellent for buffer exchange or desalting
  • Can separate molecules with at least a 10% difference in molecular weight
  • Common matrices: Sephadex, Sepharose, Superdex

Procedure

  1. Equilibrate the column with appropriate buffer
  2. Apply sample (typically 1-5% of column volume)
  3. Elute with buffer and collect fractions
  4. Monitor protein elution (UV absorbance at 280nm)
  5. Analyze fractions for protein content and purity

Important parameters:

  • Column length affects resolution
  • Flow rate affects separation quality
  • Sample viscosity should be similar to buffer

SDS-PAGE

Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate - Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) separates proteins based almost exclusively on their mass under denaturing conditions. The technique uses:

  • SDS: A detergent that denatures proteins and gives them a uniform negative charge
  • Polyacrylamide gel: Acts as a molecular sieve
  • Electric field: Drives the negatively charged proteins through the gel

Smaller proteins migrate faster through the gel matrix, while larger proteins are retarded. The electrophoretic mobility (μ) of a protein in SDS-PAGE is given by:

v = Eq/f

Where all proteins have a similar charge-to-mass ratio due to SDS binding, making mobility primarily dependent on molecular size.

SDS Explained

SDS is a key component that enables separation purely by molecular weight. It performs two critical functions:

  • Denaturation: SDS disrupts the secondary and tertiary structures of proteins, causing them to unfold into linear polypeptide chains. A reducing agent like beta-mercaptoethanol is also used to break disulfide bonds.
  • Uniform Charge: SDS molecules bind to the protein backbone at a constant ratio (about 1.4g of SDS per 1g of protein). This overwhelms the protein's intrinsic charge, giving all proteins a large, uniform negative charge proportional to their mass.

This treatment means migration in the gel depends only on molecular weight, not native charge or shape.

Electrophoretic Mobility

The velocity (v) of a protein's migration in an electric field is described by:

v = μE

Where:

Symbol Parameter Description
v Migration velocity Speed at which the protein moves through the gel
μ Electrophoretic mobility Intrinsic mobility of the protein in the gel matrix
E Electric field strength Voltage applied divided by gel length

The electrophoretic mobility (μ) itself depends on:

μ = q/f

Where q is the net charge and f is the frictional coefficient. In SDS-PAGE, since the charge-to-mass ratio is equalized, migration is therefore primarily determined by molecular size. Smaller proteins have lower frictional coefficients (f) and migrate faster.

Procedure

  1. Prepare samples with SDS and reducing agent (e.g., beta-mercaptoethanol)
  2. Heat samples to 95°C for 5 minutes to denature proteins
  3. Prepare polyacrylamide gel (concentration depends on target protein sizes)
  4. Load samples and molecular weight markers
  5. Run electrophoresis at constant voltage (100V-200V)
  6. Stain gel with Coomassie Blue or silver stain to visualize proteins

Gel concentration guide:

  • 5% gel: 200-1000 kDa
  • 10% gel: 15-70 kDa
  • 15% gel: 5-40 kDa
