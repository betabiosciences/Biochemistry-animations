Transcription Study Techniques

Section 1 of 8

1. DNase Protection Assay

Principle

Also known as DNase footprinting, this technique identifies DNA regions bound by proteins by detecting protection from DNase I digestion.

Procedure

  1. End-label DNA fragment of interest
  2. Incubate with protein extract or purified transcription factors
  3. Treat with DNase I (cleaves DNA at random positions)
  4. Separate fragments by gel electrophoresis
  5. Visualize protected regions as "footprints" (missing bands)

Applications

  • Mapping transcription factor binding sites
  • Studying protein-DNA interactions
  • Identifying promoter regions

2. G-less Cassette Assay

Principle

Measures transcription initiation by using a DNA template containing a "G-less cassette" - a region without guanine residues.

Procedure

  1. Construct plasmid with G-less cassette downstream of promoter
  2. Perform in vitro transcription with NTPs excluding GTP
  3. RNA polymerase stops at first G residue after cassette
  4. Detect transcript by incorporation of labeled nucleotides or Northern blot

Applications

  • Quantifying transcription initiation
  • Studying promoter strength
  • Analyzing effects of transcription factors

3. Run-off Transcription Assay

Principle

Measures transcription initiation by allowing RNA polymerase to "run off" the end of a linear DNA template, producing transcripts of defined length.

Procedure

  1. Linearize DNA template downstream of promoter
  2. Perform in vitro transcription with labeled nucleotides
  3. RNA polymerase falls off at DNA end
  4. Analyze transcript size by gel electrophoresis

Applications

  • Mapping transcription start sites
  • Measuring transcription rates
  • Studying promoter specificity

4. Primer Extension Method

Principle

Uses a labeled DNA primer complementary to specific mRNA, which is extended by reverse transcriptase to map the 5' end of transcripts.

Procedure

  1. Design oligonucleotide primer complementary to target mRNA
  2. Label primer with radioactivity or fluorescence
  3. Hybridize primer to mRNA
  4. Extend with reverse transcriptase
  5. Analyze extension products by gel electrophoresis

Applications

  • Mapping transcription start sites
  • Quantifying specific mRNAs
  • Detecting alternative transcription start sites

5. S1 Nuclease Mapping

Principle

Uses S1 nuclease to digest single-stranded DNA and RNA, protecting hybridized regions to map transcript ends and intron-exon boundaries.

Procedure

  1. Hybridize labeled DNA probe to RNA
  2. Treat with S1 nuclease (digests single-stranded nucleic acids)
  3. Protected DNA-RNA hybrids remain
  4. Analyze protected fragments by gel electrophoresis

Applications

  • Mapping 5' and 3' ends of mRNA
  • Identifying splice junctions
  • Quantifying specific transcripts

6. Linker Scanning Mutagenesis

Principle

Systematically introduces mutations throughout a regulatory region to identify functional elements by replacing short segments with a neutral "linker" sequence.

Procedure

  1. Create nested deletions from both ends of regulatory region
  2. Join deletion endpoints with synthetic linker
  3. Test mutant constructs in transcription assays
  4. Identify regions where mutations affect transcription

Applications

  • Identifying promoter and enhancer elements
  • Mapping functional regions in DNA
  • Studying protein-binding sites

7. Transfection Assay

Principle

Measures transcriptional activity by introducing DNA constructs containing regulatory elements and reporter genes into cells.

Procedure

  1. Clone regulatory element upstream of reporter gene (luciferase, GFP, etc.)
  2. Introduce construct into cells (transfection)
  3. Measure reporter gene expression
  4. Compare to controls to assess transcriptional activity

Applications

  • Testing promoter/enhancer activity
  • Studying transcription factor function
  • Identifying regulatory elements

8. Yeast Two Hybrid Assay

Principle

Detects protein-protein interactions by reconstituting a functional transcription factor when two proteins interact.

Procedure

  1. Fuse "bait" protein to DNA-binding domain of transcription factor
  2. Fuse "prey" protein to activation domain
  3. Co-express in yeast with reporter gene
  4. Interaction reconstitutes transcription factor, activating reporter

Applications

  • Identifying protein-protein interactions
  • Mapping interaction domains
  • Screening cDNA libraries for interacting partners
