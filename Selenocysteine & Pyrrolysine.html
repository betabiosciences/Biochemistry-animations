Selenocysteine & Pyrrolysine Synthesis

Beyond the standard 20 amino acids, nature evolved mechanisms to incorporate Selenocysteine (Sec, U) and Pyrrolysine (Pyl, O).

Selenocysteine (21st)

  • Contains Selenium (Se)
  • Encoded by UGA (Opal)
  • Found in all domains of life
  • Higher reduction potential than Cys

Pyrrolysine (22nd)

  • Contains Pyrroline ring
  • Encoded by UAG (Amber)
  • Mostly Methanogenic Archaea
  • Critical for methylamine metabolism

Key Concept: Both amino acids utilize "Stop Codon Recoding," hijacking termination signals to insert an amino acid instead of stopping translation.

Selenocysteine: Structure & Properties

Chemical Structure

Selenocysteine is essentially cysteine where the sulfur atom is replaced by selenium. The structure is H-Se-CH₂-CH(NH₂)-COOH.

pKa ~ 5.2 High Nucleophilicity Redox Active

Structural Features

  • Contains a selenium atom in place of sulfur
  • The Se-H bond is weaker than S-H, making selenocysteine more reactive
  • Selenium has a larger atomic radius than sulfur, affecting protein structure
  • The selenolate anion (R-Se-) is a stronger nucleophile than the thiolate anion (R-S-)
Selenocysteine: Biosynthesis

Biosynthetic Pathway (tRNA-Dependent)

1

tRNA Charging (Serylation)

Unique to Sec synthesis: It is not synthesized freely. First, tRNASec is acylated with Serine by Seryl-tRNA synthetase.

tRNASec + Ser + ATP → Ser-tRNASec + AMP + PPi
2

Synthesis of Selenophosphate

The Selenium donor, SePO₃³⁻, is generated by Selenophosphate synthetase (SelD) using selenide and ATP.

3

Conversion to Selenocysteine

The Serine attached to tRNA is converted directly into Selenocysteine. This mechanism varies slightly by domain:

Bacteria (SelA): Dehydrates Ser to a dehydroalanyl intermediate, then accepts Se from selenophosphate.

Archaea/Eukaryotes (SecS): Ser is first phosphorylated (by PSTK) to O-phosphoserine, then converted to Sec.

Ribosomal Incorporation (Translation)

The SECIS Element

The ribosome usually reads UGA as a stop signal. To redefine it as Selenocysteine, a structural element called the SECIS element (Selenocysteine Insertion Sequence) is required on the mRNA.

  • In Bacteria: Located immediately downstream of the UGA codon
  • In Eukaryotes: Located in the 3' Untranslated Region (3' UTR)

Specialized Elongation Factor (SelB/EF-Sec)

Standard EF-Tu cannot bind Sec-tRNASec. Instead, a specialized elongation factor (SelB in bacteria, EF-Sec in eukaryotes) is required.

Mechanism: SelB/EF-Sec binds Sec-tRNASec and simultaneously recognizes the SECIS element. This forms a quaternary complex that delivers the tRNA to the ribosomal A-site specifically at the UGA codon, preventing termination.

Pyrrolysine: Structure & Properties

Chemical Structure

Pyrrolysine is a Lysine derivative. A pyrroline ring is attached to the ε-nitrogen of Lysine via an amide bond.

Bulky Side Chain Positively Charged

Structural Features

  • Contains a pyrroline ring connected to lysine
  • The pyrroline ring can undergo chemical modifications
  • Larger and more hydrophobic than lysine
Pyrrolysine: Biosynthesis

Contrast with Sec: Pyrrolysine is synthesized as a free amino acid before attachment to tRNA.

Biosynthetic Pathway (tRNA-Independent)

1

Lysine Precursor

Two molecules of L-lysine are used as precursors for pyrrolysine biosynthesis.

2

Ring Formation

One lysine molecule forms the pyrroline ring.

3

Amide Bond Formation

The pyrroline ring is linked to the second lysine molecule via an amide bond.

4

Direct Charging (PylS)

Unlike Sec, the mature Pyl is recognized by a specific Pyrrolysyl-tRNA synthetase (PylS) and directly ligated to tRNAPyl.

Ribosomal Incorporation (Translation)

Amber Suppression (UAG)

Pyrrolysine is incorporated at the UAG (Amber) stop codon. Unlike Selenocysteine, Pyl incorporation does not require a downstream mRNA hairpin structure (like SECIS) for the codon definition, making it biologically simpler and highly valuable for genetic engineering.

Interaction with EF-Tu

The Pyl-tRNAPyl is structurally unique but is still recognized by the canonical elongation factor EF-Tu (in bacteria).

Conclusion

Selenocysteine and Pyrrolysine represent fascinating expansions of the genetic code, demonstrating nature's ability to adapt and specialize protein synthesis.

Key Comparisons:

Selenocysteine

  • One Letter Code: U
  • Genetic Code: UGA (Stop)
  • Distribution: Widespread across all domains of life
  • Incorporation: Requires specialized factors (SelB, SECIS)
  • Function: Antioxidant enzymes

Pyrrolysine

  • One Letter Code: O
  • Genetic Code: UAG (Stop)
  • Distribution: Limited to certain archaea and bacteria
  • Incorporation: Uses standard translation machinery
  • Function: Methyltransferases for methane metabolism
