RNAP I Transcription

Mechanisms & Regulation in Eukaryotes

1. Features of rRNA Genes

The eukaryotic genome requires massive amounts of ribosomes to sustain protein synthesis. Consequently, the genes coding for ribosomal RNA (rRNA) possess unique genomic features designed for high-output transcription.

Genomic Organization

  • Tandem Repeats (NORs): rRNA genes are not scattered but arranged in head-to-tail tandem arrays located at specific chromosomal regions called Nucleolar Organizer Regions (NORs). Humans have approximately 300–400 copies distributed across five acrocentric chromosomes.
  • The 45S Precursor: RNAP I transcribes a single long precursor transcript (45S pre-rRNA in mammals). This polycistronic transcript contains the sequences for 18S, 5.8S, and 28S rRNAs, separated by internal transcribed spacers (ITS) and flanked by external transcribed spacers (ETS).
  • 5S rRNA Exception: Note that the 5S rRNA is not transcribed by RNAP I; it is transcribed by RNAP III from a separate gene cluster.

Intergenic Spacers (IGS)

Individual transcription units are separated by non-coding Intergenic Spacers (IGS). Far from being "junk DNA," the IGS contains enhancer elements and terminator sequences that facilitate the rapid recycling of RNAP I, effectively "handing over" the polymerase from the end of one gene to the start of the next.

2. The RNAP I Promoter Assembly

RNAP I transcription initiation relies on a highly specific protein-DNA architecture. Unlike RNAP II, RNAP I does not require a large variety of promoters; it only recognizes one type of promoter sequence, but with extreme efficiency.

Bipartite Promoter Structure

  • Core Promoter (-45 to +20): Overlaps the Transcription Start Site (TSS). It is AT-rich (resembling a TATA box) but distinct in function. It is essential for the correct positioning of the polymerase.
  • Upstream Control Element (UCE): Located approximately 100 to 150 base pairs upstream (-150 to -100). It serves as the landing pad for regulatory factors.

The Pre-Initiation Complex (PIC)

The assembly follows a strict hierarchical order:

  1. UBF (Upstream Binding Factor): A dimer of UBF binds to both the UCE and the Core Promoter. UBF bends the DNA significantly (nearly 360° loops), bringing the UCE and Core regions into close physical proximity.
  2. SL1 (Selectivity Factor 1): Also known as TIF-IB in mice. Recruited by UBF, SL1 acts as the bridge. It contains TBP (TATA-binding protein) and several TAFs (TBP-associated factors) specific to Pol I.
  3. RNAP I Recruitment: The UBF-SL1-DNA complex creates a platform that recruits RNA Polymerase I to initiate transcription.

3. Nucleolar Architecture

The nucleolus is the site of ribosome biogenesis. It is a membrane-less organelle formed through liquid-liquid phase separation. Its structure is strictly defined by the functional stages of ribosome assembly.

Structural Compartments

  • Fibrillar Center (FC): The pale, inner core. This region contains the inactive or poised rDNA genes and stores the transcription machinery (UBF, RNAP I) when not in use.
  • Dense Fibrillar Component (DFC): A dense ring surrounding the FC. This is the site of active transcription (at the FC/DFC interface) and early pre-rRNA processing (cleavage and modification). The protein Fibrillarin is a marker for this region.
  • Granular Component (GC): The grainy outer region. This is where the late processing steps occur and where ribosomal proteins assemble onto the RNA to form the pre-ribosomal subunits (40S and 60S).

Vectorial Movement

Ribosome biogenesis involves a physical movement of molecules from the center outward:

Transcription (FC/DFC Interface) → Processing (DFC) → Assembly (GC) → Export to Cytoplasm

4. rRNA Processing Machinery

The initial 45S transcript is chemically modified and cleaved to produce mature rRNAs. This is guided by non-coding RNAs acting as scaffolds and guides.

The SSU Processome (90S)

This is a massive ribonucleoprotein complex that assembles cotranscriptionally at the 5' end of the nascent transcript. It coordinates the folding and early cleavage of the 18S rRNA component. It acts as a "chaperone" for the small ribosomal subunit.

snoRNPs (Small Nucleolar RNPs)

Modifications are directed by snoRNAs which base-pair with specific sequences on the pre-rRNA to target enzymes to that location. There are two main classes:

  • Box C/D snoRNPs:
    Function: 2'-O-Methylation (adding a methyl group to the ribose sugar).
    Catalytic Enzyme: Fibrillarin (Nop1).
    Result: Stabilizes the rRNA structure and protects it from hydrolysis.
  • Box H/ACA snoRNPs:
    Function: Pseudouridylation (isomerization of uridine to pseudouridine).
    Catalytic Enzyme: Dyskerin (Cbf5).
    Result: Enhances RNA-RNA and RNA-protein interactions within the ribosome.
