Cystic Fibrosis & Cholera

Cystic Fibrosis: Genetic Condition

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an autosomal recessive genetic disorder that affects mostly the lungs but also the pancreas, liver, kidneys, and intestine. It is caused by mutations in the gene for the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) protein.

CF Inheritance Pattern

Two carrier parents have a 25% chance of having a child with CF

The most common mutation, ΔF508, is a deletion of three nucleotides that results in a loss of the amino acid phenylalanine at the 508th position on the protein. This mutation accounts for approximately 70% of all CF cases worldwide.

CFTR Channel & Mutation Effects

The CFTR protein functions as a chloride channel across the membrane of cells that produce mucus, sweat, saliva, tears, and digestive enzymes.

CFTR Protein Structure:

  • Two Transmembrane Domains (TMD1 and TMD2): Each consisting of six alpha helices that form the channel pore through which chloride ions pass.
  • Two Nucleotide-Binding Domains (NBD1 and NBD2): Bind and hydrolyze ATP to power the gating mechanism that opens and closes the channel.
  • Regulatory (R) Domain: Contains multiple phosphorylation sites that regulate channel activity when phosphorylated by protein kinase A (PKA).
  • The protein also has intracellular N- and C-termini that play roles in regulation and trafficking.

Mutation Effects:

Mutations in the CFTR gene cause the protein to be misfolded and degraded during protein targeting, preventing it from reaching the cell membrane. Even if it reaches the membrane, its function is impaired.

CFTR Protein Structure

Two transmembrane domains • Two nucleotide-binding domains • Regulatory domain

CFTR Regulation & PKA Phosphorylation

The CFTR channel is regulated by phosphorylation of its regulatory domain by protein kinase A (PKA).

Regulatory Mechanism:

  • G-protein coupled receptors activate adenylate cyclase
  • Adenylate cyclase produces cAMP
  • cAMP activates PKA
  • PKA phosphorylates the CFTR regulatory domain
  • Phosphorylation opens the channel allowing chloride transport

In CF patients, this regulatory mechanism is disrupted due to CFTR protein dysfunction, leading to impaired chloride secretion and increased sodium absorption across epithelial membranes.

CFTR Activation Pathway

GPCR → Adenylate Cyclase → cAMP → PKA → CFTR Phosphorylation → Channel Opening

CF Effects on Body Systems

Respiratory Tract:

  • Thick, sticky mucus accumulation
  • Chronic lung infections and inflammation
  • Bronchiectasis and respiratory failure

Digestive Tract:

  • Pancreatic duct blockage leading to enzyme deficiency
  • Malabsorption and malnutrition
  • Diabetes and gastrointestinal symptoms

Other Systems:

  • Salty sweat due to impaired chloride reabsorption
  • Liver disease from bile duct obstruction
  • Infertility in males
Diagnostic Features

Elevated sweat chloride test • Chronic respiratory infections • Pancreatic insufficiency • Failure to thrive

Cholera Toxin Mechanism

Cholera is caused by the bacterium Vibrio cholerae which produces cholera toxin (CTX).

Toxin Structure:

  • One A subunit (enzymatic activity): Composed of A1 and A2 peptides linked by a disulfide bond. The A1 portion has ADP-ribosyltransferase activity.
  • Five B subunits (binding to GM1 ganglioside receptors): Form a pentameric ring that binds to GM1 gangliosides on intestinal epithelial cells.

Mechanism of Action:

The toxin binds to intestinal cells via GM1 gangliosides. The A subunit is internalized and activates ADP-ribosylation factor 6 (ARF6), a GTP-binding protein.

The A1 fragment of the cholera toxin catalyzes the ADP-ribosylation of ARF6. This modification locks ARF6 in its active GTP-bound state by inhibiting its GTPase activity, preventing the hydrolysis of GTP to GDP.

Activated ARF6 then stimulates adenylate cyclase, increasing cAMP levels dramatically. High cAMP causes massive secretion of chloride ions and water into the intestinal lumen.

Cholera Toxin Action

Toxin Binding → Internalization → ARF6 Activation → Adenylate Cyclase Activation → cAMP Increase → Chloride/Water Secretion

Clinical Features:

  • Profuse watery diarrhea ("rice-water stool")
  • Rapid dehydration and electrolyte imbalance
  • Can be fatal within hours if untreated
