Prokaryotic Transcription Termination

1. Overall Features

Termination is the final, highly regulated stage of transcription. It ensures that the RNA polymerase (RNAP) stops synthesis at the correct position, releasing both the newly made RNA transcript and the DNA template.

  • Signaling: Termination is triggered by specific sequences on the DNA template, known as terminators.
  • Dissociation: The process involves the dissociation of the entire transcription elongation complex (RNAP, DNA, and RNA).
  • Fidelity: Prevents "read-through" into downstream genes, which is crucial for correct gene expression and conserving cellular energy.
  • Two Major Pathways: Prokaryotes utilize two primary strategies for termination, defined by their protein requirements.

2. Intrinsic Termination

Also called Rho-independent, this mechanism relies *only* on the inherent sequence and structure of the RNA transcript itself. No external protein factors are required.

It is defined by two key sequence features:

  • GC-Rich Inverted Repeat: A palindromic sequence that, when transcribed, allows the RNA to fold back on itself, forming a stable stem-loop (hairpin) structure.
  • U-Rich Tract: Immediately follows the hairpin sequence. This is transcribed from an A-rich region on the DNA template strand.

Mechanism: The formation of the hairpin structure causes the RNAP to pause. This hairpin is thought to cause an allosteric change or a "jolt" to the RNAP complex, destabilizing it. During this pause, the RNAP is held to the template only by the RNA-DNA hybrid in the transcription bubble. The U-rich tract of the RNA pairs with the A-rich tract of the DNA, forming a series of A-U base pairs. This is the weakest possible base pairing (only 2 hydrogen bonds), resulting in an extremely unstable hybrid. The combination of the hairpin-induced pause and the weak A-U hybrid is sufficient to "melt" the complex, causing the RNA transcript to be released and the RNAP to dissociate from the DNA.

3. Extrinsic Termination

Also called Rho-dependent, this mechanism requires an external protein factor called Rho (ρ).

Key components:

  • Rho (ρ) Factor: A hexameric (6-subunit) protein that functions as an ATP-dependent RNA helicase and translocase.
  • `rut` Site (Rho Utilization): A C-rich, G-poor, and largely unstructured sequence on the newly synthesized RNA transcript. It serves as the loading pad for the Rho factor.

Mechanism: The Rho hexamer assembles onto the `rut` site of the nascent RNA. Using the energy from ATP hydrolysis, Rho acts as a translocase, moving along the RNA transcript in the 5' to 3' direction, "chasing" the RNAP.

RNAP eventually pauses at a downstream "Rho-dependent pause site." This pause allows Rho to catch up. Upon reaching the RNAP, Rho's helicase activity is activated, and it actively unwinds the RNA-DNA hybrid within the transcription bubble.

This "pulls" the RNA transcript out of the RNAP active site, leading to the dissociation of the entire complex.
