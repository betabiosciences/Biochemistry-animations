Structural Classification

Nonpolar Amino Acids

  • Aliphatic: Glycine (Gly, G), Alanine (Ala, A), Valine (Val, V), Leucine (Leu, L), Isoleucine (Ile, I)
  • Aromatic: Phenylalanine (Phe, F), Tryptophan (Trp, W)
  • Sulfur-containing: Methionine (Met, M)
  • Special Case: Proline (Pro, P) - cyclic structure that disrupts secondary structures

Polar Amino Acids

  • Uncharged: Serine (Ser, S), Threonine (Thr, T), Asparagine (Asn, N), Glutamine (Gln, Q), Tyrosine (Tyr, Y), Cysteine (Cys, C)
  • Acidic: Aspartate (Asp, D), Glutamate (Glu, E)
  • Basic: Lysine (Lys, K), Arginine (Arg, R), Histidine (His, H)

Key Structural Features

  • R-group properties determine protein folding and function
  • Hydrophobic residues typically buried in protein interior
  • Charged residues often found on protein surfaces
  • Cysteine forms disulfide bonds critical for tertiary structure

Nutritional Classification

Category Amino Acids Characteristics
Essential His, Ile, Leu, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, Val Cannot be synthesized by humans
Non-essential Ala, Asn, Asp, Glu, Ser Synthesized from metabolic intermediates
Conditionally Essential Arg, Cys, Gln, Gly, Pro, Tyr Required during growth, illness or trauma

Clinical Significance

Deficiencies in essential amino acids can lead to conditions like Kwashiorkor. Maple Syrup Urine Disease results from impaired metabolism of branched-chain amino acids (Leu, Ile, Val).

Metabolic Classification

  • Glucogenic: Converted to glucose via gluconeogenesis (Ala, Arg, Asn, Asp, Cys, Glu, Gln, Gly, His, Met, Pro, Ser, Val)
  • Ketogenic: Degraded to acetyl-CoA or acetoacetate (Leu, Lys)
  • Both: Can follow both pathways (Ile, Phe, Thr, Trp, Tyr)

Special Classifications & Biochemical Properties

Structural Features

  • Glycine (Gly) is the smallest and most flexible amino acid
  • Alanine (Ala) shows the highest propensity for α-helices
  • Proline (Pro) has an interlocked pyrrolidine ring structure
  • Branched-chain amino acids: Val, Leu, Ile
  • Gly and Pro are least common in secondary structures but abundant in turns

Functional Groups

  • OH group: Serine (Ser), Threonine (Thr)
  • SH group: Cysteine (Cys)
  • Amide group: Glutamine (Gln), Asparagine (Asn)
  • Mercapto group: Methionine (Met)
  • Heterocyclic: His, Trp, Pro

Acid-Base Properties

  • Most acidic: Aspartic acid (lowest pI)
  • Most basic: Arginine (highest pI)
  • Buffering capacity:: Histidine at physiological pH
  • Functional groups: His (imidazole), Lys (ε-NH₂), Arg (guanidinium)

Post-translational Modifications

  • Phosphorylation (Ser, Thr, Tyr)
  • Glycosylation (Asn, Ser, Thr)
  • Acetylation (Lys)
  • Methylation (Arg, Lys)
  • Hydroxylation (Pro, Lys)

Key Biochemical Notes

  • Aromatic amino acids absorb in the near UV range (260-280 nm)
  • Tryptophan has the highest extinction coefficient (5600 M⁻¹cm⁻¹)
  • Histidine is the only amino acid with buffering capacity at physiological pH
  • Glycine and proline are abundant in β-turns due to their flexibility and rigidity respectively
