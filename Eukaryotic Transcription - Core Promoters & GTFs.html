1. RNA Polymerases in Eukaryotes

Eukaryotes possess multiple RNA polymerases, each specialized for transcribing specific classes of genes. These enzymes are large, multi-subunit complexes that share structural similarities but have distinct functions and regulatory mechanisms.

Types of RNA Polymerases

Key Details:

  • Ion Exchange Chromatography: RNA polymerases can be separated based on their net charge. Pol I elutes first, followed by Pol II, and finally Pol III.
  • α-Amanitin: A toxic compound from death cap mushrooms that specifically inhibits Pol II at low concentrations and Pol III at higher concentrations.
  • Actinomycin D: An antibiotic that intercalates into DNA and inhibits transcription by all three RNA polymerases.

2. Detailed Structure of RNA Polymerase II

RNA Polymerase II is the most extensively studied eukaryotic RNA polymerase due to its central role in protein-coding gene expression. It consists of 12 subunits (Rpb1-Rpb12) with a total molecular weight of approximately 550 kDa.

Core Subunits and Their Functions:

  • Rpb1: Largest subunit, contains the catalytic site and C-terminal domain (CTD) with multiple heptapeptide repeats (YSPTSPS). The CTD undergoes phosphorylation during transcription initiation and elongation.
  • Rpb2: Second largest subunit, forms the active center together with Rpb1.
  • Rpb3 and Rpb11: Form a heterodimer that resembles the α subunit of bacterial RNA polymerase.
  • Rpb4 and Rpb7: Form a subcomplex that can dissociate and is involved in transcription initiation and mRNA export.
  • Rpb5, Rpb6, Rpb8, Rpb9, Rpb10, Rpb12: Common subunits shared among all three RNA polymerases.

Structural Features:

  • Active Center: Located at the base of a deep cleft, formed primarily by Rpb1 and Rpb2.
  • DNA Binding Channel: Accommodates approximately 25 base pairs of DNA during transcription.
  • RNA Exit Channel: A tunnel through which the nascent RNA transcript exits the polymerase.
  • C-Terminal Domain (CTD): Unique to Pol II, consists of multiple repeats (26 in yeast, 52 in humans) of the heptapeptide sequence YSPTSPS. The phosphorylation state of these repeats regulates transcription and co-transcriptional processing.

3. RNA Polymerase II Core Promoter Elements

The core promoter is the minimal DNA sequence required to accurately initiate transcription. It typically spans from approximately -40 to +40 relative to the transcription start site (TSS).

Key Core Promoter Elements:

  • TATA Box: Consensus sequence TATA(A/T)A(A/T), located ~25-35 bp upstream of TSS. Recognized by TBP (TATA-binding protein) subunit of TFIID.
  • Inr (Initiator): Encompasses the TSS with consensus sequence YYAN(T/A)YY (where Y = pyrimidine).
  • DPE (Downstream Promoter Element): Located ~28-32 bp downstream of TSS with consensus sequence (A/G)G(A/T)(C/T)(G/A/C). Often found in TATA-less promoters.
  • DCE (Downstream Core Element): Comprises three subelements (I, II, III) located downstream of TSS.
  • MTE (Motif Ten Element): Located ~18-27 bp downstream of TSS, enhances transcription.
  • CpG Islands: Regions with high frequency of CpG dinucleotides, often associated with gene promoters, especially housekeeping genes.

CpG Islands and DNA Methylation:

CpG islands are genomic regions with high GC content and a high frequency of CpG dinucleotides. They are typically 0.5-2 kb in length and are often found in promoter regions.

  • Methylation: DNA methyltransferases add methyl groups to cytosine bases in CpG dinucleotides, forming 5-methylcytosine.
  • Transcriptional Regulation: Methylation of CpG islands in promoter regions generally leads to gene silencing by preventing transcription factor binding and recruiting repressive chromatin modifiers.
  • Bisulfite Sequencing: A technique used to identify methylated CpG sites. Treatment with bisulfite converts unmethylated cytosines to uracils (which are read as thymines in sequencing), while methylated cytosines remain unchanged.

Focused vs. Dispersed Promoters

Eukaryotic promoters can be broadly classified into two categories based on their transcription start site distribution:

  • Focused Promoters:
    • Contain a single, well-defined transcription start site (TSS)
    • Often associated with tissue-specific or developmentally regulated genes
    • Typically contain strong core promoter elements like TATA boxes
    • Produce precise transcripts with defined 5' ends
    • Examples: β-globin gene, many viral promoters
  • Dispersed Promoters:
    • Contain multiple transcription start sites spread over 50-100 nucleotides
    • Often associated with housekeeping genes and genes involved in basic cellular functions
    • Typically lack TATA boxes but often contain CpG islands
    • Produce transcripts with heterogeneous 5' ends
    • Examples: Most mammalian housekeeping genes, such as those encoding metabolic enzymes

The distinction between focused and dispersed promoters reflects different regulatory strategies - focused promoters allow precise control of gene expression, while dispersed promoters provide more robust, consistent expression across different cell types and conditions.

4. General Transcription Factors (GTFs)

General Transcription Factors are proteins that assemble at the core promoter to facilitate the recruitment and proper positioning of RNA Polymerase II for transcription initiation.

TFIID

  • Composition: Multisubunit complex containing TBP (TATA-binding protein) and 13-14 TBP-associated factors (TAFs).
  • Function: Recognizes and binds core promoter elements (TATA box, Inr, DPE). Serves as a platform for assembly of the pre-initiation complex.

TFIIA

  • Composition: Heterotrimeric complex.
  • Function: Stabilizes TBP binding to DNA, counteracts inhibitory factors, and assists in promoter recognition.

TFIIB

  • Composition: Single polypeptide.
  • Function: Bridges TFIID and Pol II, helps determine transcription start site, and recruits Pol II-TFIIF complex.

TFIIF

  • Composition: Heterotetramer (RAP74, RAP30, and two smaller subunits).
  • Function: Escorts Pol II to the pre-initiation complex, stabilizes Pol II binding, and plays a role in both initiation and elongation.

TFIIE

  • Composition: Heterodimer (α and β subunits).
  • Function: Recruits and regulates TFIIH, helps open the promoter DNA, and transitions from initiation to elongation.

TFIIH

  • Composition: 10-subunit complex with kinase (CDK7) and helicase (XPB, XPD) activities.
  • Function: Phosphorylates Pol II CTD, unwinds DNA at promoter (helicase activity), and plays roles in both transcription and DNA repair (nucleotide excision repair).

Pre-initiation Complex Assembly:

  1. TFIID binds to core promoter elements
  2. TFIIA and TFIIB join the complex
  3. Pol II-TFIIF complex is recruited
  4. TFIIE and TFIIH join to complete the pre-initiation complex
  5. TFIIH phosphorylates Pol II CTD and unwinds DNA
  6. Transcription initiation begins
