Gq-Coupled Protein Signaling Pathway

1Ligand Binding

A signaling molecule (ligand) binds to the extracellular domain of a Gq protein-coupled receptor (GPCR).

Ligand GPCR

This binding induces a conformational change in the receptor, activating it and enabling it to interact with the Gq protein.

2G Protein Activation

The activated receptor facilitates the exchange of GDP for GTP on the Gαq subunit of the Gq protein.

Gαq Gβγ GTP

This exchange causes the Gq protein to dissociate into Gαq-GTP and Gβγ subunits, both of which can regulate downstream effectors.

3PLC Activation

The Gαq-GTP subunit binds to and activates the enzyme phospholipase C-β (PLC-β).

PLC-β PIP₂

Activated PLC-β hydrolyzes phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP₂) in the plasma membrane.

4Second Messenger Generation

Hydrolysis of PIP₂ produces two second messengers: inositol trisphosphate (IP₃) and diacylglycerol (DAG).

IP₃ DAG

These second messengers initiate two distinct signaling pathways that converge to produce the cellular response.

5Calcium Release

IP₃ diffuses through the cytosol and binds to IP₃ receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum (ER).

IP₃ Receptor Ca²⁺

This binding causes the release of calcium ions (Ca²⁺) from ER stores into the cytoplasm, increasing intracellular calcium concentration.

6PKC Activation

DAG remains in the membrane and recruits protein kinase C (PKC) along with the increased calcium levels.

PKC Ca²⁺

The combination of DAG and calcium ions activates PKC, which then phosphorylates various target proteins to alter their activity.

7Cellular Response

The increased calcium levels and PKC activation lead to various cellular responses depending on the cell type.

Gene Expression Secretion Contraction Metabolism

These responses can include muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, hormone secretion, gene expression changes, and metabolic adjustments.

8Signal Termination

The Gαq subunit has intrinsic GTPase activity that hydrolyzes GTP to GDP.

GTPase RGS

This hydrolysis inactivates Gαq, allowing it to reassociate with Gβγ subunits. Regulators of G protein signaling (RGS) proteins accelerate GTP hydrolysis. Calcium is pumped back into the ER and out of the cell, and second messengers are metabolized.
