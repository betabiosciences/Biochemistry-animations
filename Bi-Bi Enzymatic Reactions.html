Bi-Bi Enzymatic Reactions

The Challenge of Two Substrates

Most enzymatic reactions in biological systems involve two substrates and yield two products (Bi-Bi reactions). Studying their kinetics is far more complex than for single-substrate reactions.

The Core Problem: With two changing variables (the concentration of substrate A, [A], and substrate B, [B]), it's difficult to determine the true effect of each on the reaction rate.

The Solution: To simplify this, we fix the concentration of one substrate at a saturating level (making it a pseudo-constant) and then vary the concentration of the other. By repeating this process at several fixed concentrations of the first substrate, we can build a complete kinetic profile and determine the reaction mechanism.

1. Single Displacement (Sequential) Reactions

In sequential mechanisms, both substrates must bind to the enzyme before any product is released. This forms a central ternary complex, which contains the enzyme and both substrates (EAB).

The general reaction pathway is:

E + A + B ⇌ EAB ⇌ EPQ ⇌ E + P + Q

There are two main subtypes for how the ternary complex can be formed: Ordered and Random.

1a. Ordered Single Displacement

Substrates bind in a compulsory order. Substrate A (the leading substrate) must bind first, creating a binding site for substrate B. Products are also released in a defined order.

E + A ⇌ EA + B ⇌ EAB ⇌ EPQ ⇌ EQ + P ⇌ E + Q

Kinetic Effects:

  • Apparent KM: The KM for one substrate is dependent on the concentration of the other.
  • Vmax: The maximum velocity also changes as the concentration of the co-substrate changes.

Lineweaver-Burk Plot:

The plot shows lines that intersect to the left of the 1/V axis. Each line represents a different fixed concentration of the non-varied substrate.

1b. Random Single Displacement

The substrates can bind in any order to the enzyme. Both paths lead to the formation of the same EAB ternary complex.

Kinetic Effects & Plot:

Kinetically, this mechanism is often indistinguishable from the ordered mechanism using initial rate studies alone. The Lineweaver-Burk plot also shows intersecting lines to the left of the 1/V axis, which can look identical to the ordered mechanism. Differentiating them requires other techniques like product inhibition studies.

2. Double Displacement (Ping-Pong) Reactions

In this mechanism, one substrate binds and one product is released before the second substrate binds. This creates a stable, modified enzyme intermediate (F). No ternary complex is formed.

E + A ⇌ EA ⇌ FP - P → F + B ⇌ FB ⇌ EQ - Q → E

Kinetic Effects:

  • Apparent KM: Increases as [B] increases.
  • Vmax: Increases as [B] increases.
  • The ratio of Apparent KM / Vmax remains constant.

Lineweaver-Burk Plot:

This mechanism is easily identified by its distinctive plot, which shows a series of parallel lines.
