Protein Targeting to Peroxisomes

Introduction to Peroxisomes

Peroxisomes are essential organelles found in virtually all eukaryotic cells. They play crucial roles in lipid metabolism, reactive oxygen species detoxification, and biosynthesis of important cellular components.

Key Functions:

  • Beta-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids
  • Synthesis of plasmalogens (important membrane phospholipids)
  • Detoxification of reactive oxygen species (ROS)
  • Metabolism of purines and polyamines

Unlike many organelles, peroxisomes can import fully folded and even oligomeric proteins. This unique feature requires specialized targeting and import mechanisms.

Peroxisome Structure

Peroxisomes consist of a single membrane surrounding a dense matrix containing various enzymes. The membrane contains specific proteins that facilitate the import of matrix proteins and perform other functions.

Peroxisomal Targeting Signals

Proteins destined for peroxisomes contain specific targeting signals that direct them to the organelle. The two best-characterized signals are PTS1 and PTS2.

PTS1 (Peroxisomal Targeting Signal 1)

The PTS1 is a C-terminal tripeptide with the consensus sequence Ser-Lys-Leu (SKL) or a conservative variant. This signal is recognized by the cytosolic receptor Pex5p.

PTS1 is the most common peroxisomal targeting signal, used by approximately 80% of peroxisomal matrix proteins. The signal is both necessary and sufficient for targeting proteins to peroxisomes.

PTS2 (Peroxisomal Targeting Signal 2)

The PTS2 is an N-terminal nonapeptide with the consensus sequence (R/K)(L/V/I)X5(H/Q)(L/A). This signal is recognized by the receptor Pex7p.

Unlike PTS1, the PTS2 signal is cleaved from the protein after import into the peroxisome. This signal is used by a smaller subset of peroxisomal matrix proteins.

Some peroxisomal membrane proteins (PMPs) use different targeting signals that are recognized by specialized receptors.

PTS1 Import Pathway

The PTS1 pathway is the major route for peroxisomal matrix protein import. It involves a sophisticated receptor-cargo shuttle system.

The PTS1 Receptor: PEX5

PEX5 is the cytosolic receptor for PTS1-containing cargo proteins. It is the central element of the matrix import pathway.

Binding and Import Cycle

  • Cargo Loading: PEX5 binds the PTS1-containing cargo protein in the cytosol.
  • Docking: The PEX5-cargo complex docks at the peroxisomal membrane on the PEX13/PEX14 complex (the docking complex).
  • Translocation: PEX5 acts as a mobile receptor that physically translocates the cargo through the membrane.

The Importomer Complex (Translocation)

The translocation machinery is a multiprotein complex known as the importomer (or peroxisomal translocon). Key components involved in both PTS1 and PTS2 import:

Core Components

  • PEX14: The central component of the docking site, binding PEX5 (and the PTS2 receptor, PEX7).
  • PEX13: A membrane protein that also contributes to the docking and forms part of the translocation pore.
  • PEX10, PEX12, PEX2: These are RING finger peroxins, integral membrane proteins that collectively form the ubiquitination/E3 ligase complex essential for the next step: receptor recycling.

The PEX5-cargo complex transiently forms a pore structure with PEX14/PEX13 large enough to pass the folded, oligomeric cargo proteins, a unique feature distinguishing it from ER/Mitochondrial import.

PEX5 Receptor Recycling and Disassembly

After releasing its cargo into the matrix, PEX5 must be removed from the membrane and recycled back to the cytosol. This is an ATP-dependent process driven by AAA+ ATPases.

Key Steps and Proteins

  • Ubiquitination: The import complex (PEX10/12/2) monoubiquitinates PEX5 at the membrane.
  • AAA+ ATPase Action: The complex of two cytosolic ATPases, PEX1 and PEX6, forms a heterohexamer (PEX1/PEX6) that recognizes ubiquitinated PEX5, binding via the adapter protein PEX26.
  • Energy Dependence: Hydrolysis of ATP by PEX1 and PEX6 powers the extraction of PEX5 from the peroxisomal membrane, pulling it back into the cytosol for the next round of import.

A failure in this recycling step (e.g., in Zellweger syndrome) leads to the membrane being clogged with PEX5, halting new protein import despite the membrane proteins being present.

PTS2 Import Pathway

The PTS2 pathway imports a smaller subset of peroxisomal matrix proteins and shares components with the PTS1 pathway.

Key Components:

  • Pex7p: Cytosolic receptor that recognizes PTS2 signals
  • Pex18p/Pex21p: Co-receptors in yeast that facilitate Pex7p function
  • Pex14p: Docking protein shared with the PTS1 pathway

Import Mechanism:

Pex7p binds PTS2-containing proteins in the cytosol
The complex associates with co-receptors (species-dependent)
Docking occurs at the peroxisomal membrane via Pex14p
Cargo is translocated into the peroxisomal matrix
PTS2 signal is cleaved from the imported protein
Pex7p is recycled back to the cytosol

In mammals, Pex7p function requires additional co-factors for efficient PTS2 protein import. The PTS2 pathway shares many components with the PTS1 pathway but maintains distinct features.
