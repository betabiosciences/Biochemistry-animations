Reversible Enzyme Inhibitions

What is Reversible Inhibition?

Reversible enzyme inhibition occurs when an inhibitor binds to an enzyme in a non-covalent manner, forming an equilibrium between the enzyme-inhibitor complex and free enzyme. Unlike irreversible inhibition, the inhibitor can dissociate from the enzyme, allowing the enzyme to regain its catalytic activity. This type of inhibition is characterized by the inhibitor's ability to compete with or modify the binding of substrate molecules.

Key Characteristics:
  • Non-covalent binding between inhibitor and enzyme
  • Equilibrium established between bound and free states
  • Inhibition can be overcome by increasing substrate concentration (in some cases)
  • Follows equilibrium kinetics with dissociation constants

Types of Reversible Inhibitions

🔄 Competitive Inhibition

Inhibitor competes with substrate for the same binding site on the enzyme

🔗 Uncompetitive Inhibition

Inhibitor binds only to the enzyme-substrate complex

⚖️ Mixed Inhibition

Inhibitor can bind to both free enzyme and enzyme-substrate complex

Mechanism of Competitive Inhibition

In competitive inhibition, the inhibitor (I) competes with the substrate (S) for the same active site on the enzyme (E). The inhibitor and substrate cannot bind simultaneously because they compete for the same binding site.

E + S ⇌ ES → E + P
E + I ⇌ EI (inactive)
Key Features:
• Inhibitor binds to free enzyme only
• Can be overcome by increasing [S]
• Km increases, Vmax unchanged
• Inhibitor often structurally similar to substrate

Mechanism of Uncompetitive Inhibition

In uncompetitive inhibition, the inhibitor binds only to the enzyme-substrate complex (ES), not to the free enzyme. This type of inhibition cannot be overcome by increasing substrate concentration.

E + S ⇌ ES → E + P
ES + I ⇌ ESI (inactive)
Key Features:
• Inhibitor binds to ES complex only
• Cannot be overcome by increasing [S]
• Both Km and Vmax decrease
• Rare in single-substrate reactions

Mechanism of Mixed Inhibition

In mixed inhibition, the inhibitor can bind to both the free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex, but with different affinities. This is the most general case of reversible inhibition.

E + S ⇌ ES → E + P
E + I ⇌ EI
ES + I ⇌ ESI
EI + S ⇌ ESI
Key Features:
• Inhibitor binds to both E and ES
• Different binding affinities for E and ES
• Both Km and Vmax affected
• Most common type in physiological systems

Dixon Plot Analysis

The Dixon plot is a graphical method for determining the type and mechanism of enzyme inhibition. It plots 1/v versus [I] at different fixed substrate concentrations.

Dixon Plot Characteristics:
• Competitive: Lines intersect on the y-axis (1/v axis)
• Uncompetitive: Lines are parallel
• Non-competitive: Lines intersect on the x-axis
• Mixed: Lines intersect above or below the x-axis
