Stages in Eukaryotic Transcription

Stage 1: Initiation

1. Closed Chromatin (Heterochromatin)

Gene is inaccessible, DNA is tightly wound around histones.

↓

2. Open Chromatin (Euchromatin)

Activator proteins recruit chromatin remodelers (e.g., SWI/SNF) and Histone Acetyltransferases (HATs) to expose the promoter region.

↓

3. Pre-Initiation Complex (PIC) Assembly

General Transcription Factors (GTFs) assemble. TFIID (with TBP) binds the TATA box, followed by TFIIA, B, Pol II/TFIIF, E, and H.

↓

4. Closed Promoter Complex

The complete PIC is assembled on the promoter, but the DNA remains double-stranded.

↓

5. Open Promoter Complex

The helicase activity of TFIIH unwinds the DNA at the transcription start site, creating the "transcription bubble."

↓

6. Abortive Initiation

Pol II synthesizes and releases short RNA transcripts (2-10 nucleotides) without moving from the promoter. This "scrunching" builds energy.

↓

7. Promoter Clearance / Escape

Pol II breaks its tethers to the promoter. Requires TFIIH kinase activity to phosphorylate Serine 5 (Ser5) on the Pol II C-Terminal Domain (CTD).

↓

8. Promoter-Proximal Pausing

Pol II stalls ~30-60 bp downstream. Mediated by DSIF and NELF (Negative Elongation Factor). Allows for 5' capping.

↓

9. Super Elongation Complex (SEC) Assembly

P-TEFb (CDK9/CycT1) is recruited. It phosphorylates NELF (causing its release) and DSIF, converting it into a positive factor. P-TEFb also phosphorylates Serine 2 (Ser2) on the CTD.

↓

10. Productive Elongation

Pol II is released from pausing and begins processive synthesis of the full-length RNA.

Stage 2: Elongation

Once released from pausing, Pol II moves processively along the DNA template. The Ser2 phosphorylation of its CTD is a hallmark of this stage, serving as a docking site for RNA processing factors.

Elongation Factor: TFIIS

TFIIS acts as a "proofreading" and rescue factor. When Pol II encounters a difficult sequence or incorporates a wrong nucleotide, it can backtrack, pushing the new RNA 3' end out of the active site.

TFIIS re-aligns the active site by stimulating Pol II's intrinsic ribonuclease activity, cleaving the backtracked RNA and creating a new 3' end, allowing synthesis to resume.

Elongation Factor: FACT

FACT (Facilitates Chromatin Transcription) is a histone chaperone complex essential for transcription through nucleosomes.

As Pol II approaches a nucleosome, FACT (composed of SPT16 and SSRP1) partially disassembles it by removing one H2A/H2B dimer. This allows Pol II to pass through. Behind the polymerase, FACT reassembles the nucleosome, maintaining chromatin integrity.

Stage 3: Termination

Termination of Pol II transcription is complex and tightly linked to the 3' end processing (cleavage and polyadenylation) of the new mRNA.

Polyadenylation-Coupled "Torpedo" Model

1. Signal Recognition: Pol II transcribes past the poly(A) signal (AAUAAA) and a downstream G/U-rich element. These signals are recognized on the nascent RNA by CPSF (Cleavage and Polyadenylation Specificity Factor) and CstF (Cleavage Stimulation Factor), which ride on the Pol II CTD.

2. RNA Cleavage: The RNA is cleaved between the two signals. The "upstream" part becomes the pre-mRNA and is rapidly polyadenylated by Poly(A) Polymerase (PAP).

3. Torpedo Recruitment: The "downstream" part of the RNA remains attached to the transcribing Pol II. Its 5' end is now uncapped and exposed.

4. Torpedo Action: A 5'-to-3' exonuclease (Xrn2 in humans) binds to this uncapped 5' end and begins rapidly degrading the RNA, "chasing" the polymerase.

5. Dissociation: When the Xrn2 "torpedo" catches up to Pol II, it is thought to trigger a conformational change that dislodges the polymerase from the DNA template, terminating transcription.
