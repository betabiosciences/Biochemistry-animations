Myoglobin: Oxygen Storage Protein

Structural Features

Helical Composition

Myoglobin consists of 8 α-helices (A-H) arranged to form a compact globular structure.

  • Approximately 75% of the polypeptide chain is helical
  • Helices range from 7 to 24 amino acids in length
  • Stabilized by hydrogen bonds between carbonyl oxygen and amide hydrogen
  • Hydrophobic core protects the heme group from solvent

Tertiary Structure

The 3D structure is crucial for myoglobin's function:

  • Compact globular structure (45Å × 35Å × 25Å)
  • Hydrophobic interior with hydrophilic exterior
  • Heme group nestled in a hydrophobic pocket
  • Only two polar residues in heme pocket: His64 (distal) and His93 (proximal)
  • Stabilized by hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, and van der Waals forces

Oxygen Binding Site

The active site contains the heme group where oxygen binds:

  • Buried deep within the protein structure
  • Accessible through a channel between helices E and F
  • Proximal histidine (His93) coordinates with Fe²⁺
  • Distal histidine (His64) stabilizes bound oxygen
  • Hydrophobic environment prevents oxidation of Fe²⁺

Heme Group Structure

Protoporphyrin IX

The heme group is a prosthetic group composed of:

  • Organic component: Protoporphyrin IX
  • Central iron atom (Fe²⁺) in ferrous state
  • Four pyrrole rings linked by methine bridges
  • Substituents: methyl, vinyl, and propionate groups
  • Planar structure with iron at center

Iron Coordination

The iron atom has six coordination sites:

  • Four sites occupied by pyrrole nitrogen atoms
  • Fifth site coordinated by proximal histidine (His93)
  • Sixth site binds molecular oxygen (O₂)
  • Distal histidine (His64) stabilizes bound oxygen
  • Iron lies slightly out of porphyrin plane in deoxy state

Oxygen Binding Mechanism

Conformational Changes

Oxygen binding induces subtle but important conformational changes:

  • Iron atom moves into porphyrin plane upon O₂ binding (0.3Å shift)
  • Proximal histidine (His93) is pulled toward porphyrin ring
  • Bound O₂ forms hydrogen bond with distal histidine (His64)
  • O₂ binding is stabilized by hydrophobic pocket environment
  • No significant quaternary structure changes (unlike hemoglobin)

Deoxy-Myoglobin

  • Fe²⁺ in high-spin state
  • Larger ionic radius
  • Iron positioned 0.3Å above porphyrin plane
  • His93 imidazole ring tilted
  • O₂ binding site vacant

Oxy-Myoglobin

  • Fe²⁺ in low-spin state
  • Smaller ionic radius
  • Iron moves into porphyrin plane
  • His93 imidazole ring shifts position
  • O₂ bound at angle to Fe-O-O bond

Fractional Saturation

The fractional saturation (Y) represents the fraction of myoglobin molecules bound to oxygen:

$Y = \frac{[pO_2]}{P_{50} + [pO_2]}$

Where:

  • Y = Fraction of oxygen-binding sites occupied
  • [pO₂] = Partial pressure of oxygen (mmHg or torr)
  • P₅₀ = Partial pressure at which myoglobin is half-saturated

Myoglobin has a P₅₀ of 2.8 torr, meaning it becomes half-saturated at very low oxygen pressures, making it ideal for oxygen storage in muscle tissue.

Key observations:

  • Myoglobin exhibits hyperbolic oxygen binding curve
  • Hemoglobin exhibits sigmoidal curve due to cooperativity
  • Myoglobin has much higher affinity for oxygen than hemoglobin
  • At low pO₂ (e.g., in muscle tissue), myoglobin binds oxygen released by hemoglobin
  • Myoglobin serves as an oxygen reserve during periods of oxygen deprivation
