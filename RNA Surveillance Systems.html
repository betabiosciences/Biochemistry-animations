RNA Surveillance Mechanisms in Protein Translation

Section 1 of 4

1. trans-translation in Prokaryotes

tmRNA-Mediated Ribosome Rescue

trans-translation is a vital quality control mechanism in bacteria that rescues ribosomes stalled on non-stop or damaged mRNAs. This process is unique to prokaryotes and serves as the primary surveillance system for translational arrest.

Key Molecular Players:

  • tmRNA (ssrA RNA): A bifunctional RNA with both tRNA-like and mRNA-like domains
  • SmpB: Small protein B, essential for tmRNA function
  • Alanyl-tRNA synthetase (AlaRS): Charges tmRNA with alanine
  • EF-Tu: Elongation factor that delivers tmRNA to the ribosome

The trans-translation Process:

Step 1: Ribosome stalls at the 3' end of an mRNA lacking a stop codon or encounters a damaged mRNA
Step 2: tmRNA•SmpB complex recognizes the stalled ribosome and binds to the A site
Step 3: The nascent polypeptide chain is transferred to the alanine on tmRNA (peptidyl transfer)
Step 4: Ribosome switches templates from the original mRNA to the internal reading frame of tmRNA
Step 5: Translation continues to add a C-terminal ANDENYALAA degradation tag
Step 6: Tagged protein is released and rapidly degraded by AAA+ proteases
Step 7: The damaged mRNA is released and degraded by RNases

Biological Significance:

  • Prevents sequestration of ribosomes by defective mRNAs
  • Eliminates potentially toxic truncated proteins
  • Recycles amino acids through proteolysis
  • Contributes to bacterial stress responses
  • Essential for virulence in many pathogenic bacteria
  • Quality control for ribosome biogenesis

2. Non-sense Mediated Decay (NMD)

Eukaryotic Surveillance for Premature Termination Codons

Nonsense-Mediated Decay (NMD) is a sophisticated eukaryotic mechanism that degrades mRNAs containing premature termination codons (PTCs), preventing the production of truncated, potentially harmful proteins. This pathway integrates with translation and mRNA export to ensure transcript quality.

Core NMD Factors:

  • UPF1 (Up-frameshift protein 1): ATP-dependent RNA helicase, central regulator
  • UPF2: Adaptor protein that bridges UPF1 and UPF3
  • UPF3A/UPF3B: RNA-binding proteins that interact with EJC
  • SMG1 (Suppressor with morphological defects in genitalia 1): PI3K-like kinase that phosphorylates UPF1
  • SMG5, SMG6, SMG7: Effectors that promote mRNA decapping and degradation
  • Exon Junction Complex (EJC): Deposited upstream of splicing junctions

NMD Activation Mechanism:

Step 1: Splicing deposits EJCs 20-24 nucleotides upstream of exon-exon junctions
Step 2: Translation termination occurs at a stop codon located >50-55 nt upstream of an EJC (the "50-55 nucleotide rule")
Step 3: Terminating ribosome recruits UPF1-SMG1 complex via eRF1/eRF3 interaction
Step 4: UPF1 interacts with EJC-bound UPF2-UPF3, forming the SURF complex
Step 5: UPF1 is phosphorylated by SMG1, triggering NMD cascade
Step 6: Phosphorylated UPF1 recruits SMG5-SMG7 and SMG6
Step 7a (SMG6): Endonucleolytic cleavage 5' to the EJC
Step 7b (SMG5-SMG7): Recruitment of decapping complex (DCP1/DCP2) and deadenylases
Step 8: 5'→3' degradation by XRN1 or 3'→5' degradation by exosome

Physiological Importance:

  • Eliminates transcripts with nonsense mutations
  • Controls expression of alternatively spliced isoforms
  • Eliminates transcripts with upstream open reading frames (uORFs)
  • Prevents accumulation of PTC-containing mRNA from programmed frameshifting
  • Linked to cancer, neurodevelopmental disorders, and autoimmune diseases

3. Non-stop Mediated Decay (NSD)

Ribosome Rescue at the 3' mRNA End

Non-stop Mediated Decay (NSD) is an eukaryotic quality control pathway that targets mRNAs lacking in-frame stop codons. Similar to bacterial trans-translation, NSD rescues ribosomes stalled at the 3' end of mRNA and triggers degradation of both the defective mRNA and the incomplete polypeptide.

Key NSD Factors:

  • Dom34 (Pelota in mammals): Ribosome rescue factor, paralog of eRF1
  • Hbs1 (Hsp70 subfamily B suppressor 1): GTPase, paralog of eRF3
  • Ski complex: Helicase complex for 3'→5' degradation
  • Exosome: Multi-subunit 3'→5' exoribonuclease
  • Xrn1: Major cytoplasmic 5'→3' exoribonuclease
  • E3 ubiquitin ligases: Tag incomplete proteins for proteasomal degradation

NSD Pathway Steps:

Step 1: Ribosome reaches the 3' end of poly(A) tail without encountering a stop codon
Step 2: Stalled ribosome is recognized by Dom34•Hbs1•GTP complex
Step 3: Dom34•Hbs1 binds to the empty A site, mimicking termination complex
Step 4: Dom34•Hbs1•GTP promotes peptidyl-tRNA hydrolysis and ribosome disassembly
Step 5: Incomplete polypeptide is released with C-terminal tRNA attached
Step 6: Listerin E3 ligase recognizes stalled ribosomes and ubiquitinates nascent chains
Step 7: Tagged proteins are degraded by the proteasome
Step 8: Non-stop mRNA targets for degradation:
  • Endonucleolytic cleavage near stalled ribosome
  • 3'→5' degradation by exosome-associated Ski complex
  • 5'→3' degradation by Xrn1 after decapping

Physiological Functions:

  • Rescues ribosomes from non-stop mRNAs arising from:
    • Premature transcription termination
    • Polyadenylation within coding sequence
    • Truncated mRNA fragments
    • Endonucleolytic cleavage of mRNAs
  • Prevents accumulation of stalled ribosomes that could deplete translation machinery
  • Eliminates incomplete proteins lacking functional C-terminal domains
  • Interacts with no-go decay (NGD) pathway for comprehensive surveillance
  • Critical during stress conditions when transcription fidelity decreases
  • Quality control for defective mRNA nuclear export

4. No-Go Mediated Decay (NGD)

Resolution of Ribosome Stalling During Elongation

No-Go Mediated Decay (NGD) is an eukaryotic mRNA surveillance pathway that responds to ribosomes stalled during translation elongation due to mRNA damage, stable secondary structures, or rare codon clusters. Unlike NSD, NGD handles stalls within the coding sequence and initiates endonucleolytic cleavage of the problematic mRNA.

NGD Molecular Mechanism:

Step 1: Ribosome stalls due to:
  • Stable RNA secondary structures (hairpins, pseudoknots)
  • Chemical damage to mRNA (oxidation, UV-induced lesions)
  • Clusters of rare codons (tRNA scarcity)
  • Strong positive charge in nascent chain (poly-arginine)
  • Drug-induced inhibition (e.g., cycloheximide)
Step 2: Following ribosomes collide with stalled ribosome, creating disome
Step 3: Ribosome collision is detected and ubiquitinates a ribosomal protein
Step 4: Dom34/Hbs1 is recruited to the ubiquitinated ribosome
Step 5: Endonucleolytic cleavage of mRNA near the stall site (3' side of stalled ribosome)
Step 6: Ribosome recycling by Dom34/Hbs1.
Step 7: mRNA fragments degraded:
  • 5' fragment: degraded by Xrn1 after decapping
  • 3' fragment: degraded by exosome assisted by Ski complex
Step 8: Nascent polypeptide ubiquitinated and degraded by proteasome
