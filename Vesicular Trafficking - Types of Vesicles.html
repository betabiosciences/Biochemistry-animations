Vesicular Trafficking

Section 1: Overview of Vesicular Trafficking

Overall Features

Vesicular trafficking is a fundamental cellular process responsible for transporting molecules between different cellular compartments. This system ensures proper distribution of proteins, lipids, and other macromolecules to maintain cellular organization and function.

Key Destinations

  • Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) - Site of protein synthesis and initial processing
  • Golgi Apparatus - Protein modification, sorting, and packaging center
  • Plasma Membrane - Delivery of membrane proteins and secretion
  • Endosomes/Lysosomes - Degradation and recycling pathways

Components in a Budding Vesicle

  • Cargo - Molecules being transported (proteins, lipids)
  • Coat Proteins - Form a scaffold to shape the vesicle (COPII, COPI, clathrin)
  • SNAREs - Mediate vesicle docking and fusion (v-SNAREs on vesicles, t-SNAREs on target membranes)
  • G-proteins - Regulate vesicle formation and uncoating (ARF, Sar1, Rab families)
  • Adaptor Proteins - Link cargo receptors to coat proteins
  • Dynamin - Pinches off vesicles from donor membranes

Section 2: COPII Vesicles

Coat Proteins

COPII vesicles are characterized by a specific set of coat proteins:

  • Sec23/Sec24 - Inner coat complex that selects cargo
  • Sec13/Sec31 - Outer coat complex forming polyhedral lattice
  • Sec16 - Scaffolding protein that organizes COPII assembly

G-proteins

Sar1 is the key regulatory G-protein for COPII vesicle formation:

  • Activated by Sec12 (GEF) at the ER membrane
  • GTP-bound Sar1 recruits the COPII coat
  • Inactivated by GAP activity of Sec23

Mechanism of Vesicle Budding

  1. Sar1-GDP is recruited to ER membrane and activated by Sec12 (GEF)
  2. Sar1-GTP inserts into membrane, causing curvature
  3. Sar1 recruits Sec23/Sec24 complex (inner coat)
  4. Sec23/Sec24 binds cargo and recruits Sec13/Sec31 (outer coat)
  5. Coat assembly drives membrane curvature to form vesicle
  6. Vesicle pinches off and uncoats before fusion

Vesicle Docking and Fusion Mechanism

  • Rab GTPases direct vesicles to correct target membranes
  • v-SNAREs on vesicles pair with t-SNAREs on target membranes
  • SNARE complex formation brings membranes into close proximity
  • Membrane fusion occurs, releasing cargo into target compartment

Section 3: Vesicle Fusion Mechanism

v-SNARE and t-SNARE Interactions

The specificity and energy for vesicle fusion is provided by SNARE proteins:

  • v-SNAREs - Located on vesicle membranes (e.g., VAMP/synaptobrevin)
  • t-SNAREs - Located on target membranes (e.g., syntaxin, SNAP-25)
  • Complementary v- and t-SNAREs recognize each other with high specificity
  • Rab GTPases and tethering factors help bring vesicles close to target membranes

Four-Helix Bundle Formation

The core fusion machinery consists of a tightly packed four-helix bundle:

  • One helix contributed by v-SNARE (VAMP)
  • One helix contributed by t-SNARE (syntaxin)
  • Two helices contributed by t-SNARE (SNAP-25)
  • Bundle formation proceeds from N- to C-terminus (zipper mechanism)
  • This zippering provides the energy to overcome repulsion between membranes

Trans-SNARE and Cis-SNARE Complexes

  • Trans-SNARE Complex - Forms when v- and t-SNAREs from different membranes interact
  • Trans-SNARE complex pulls membranes together, leading to fusion
  • Cis-SNARE Complex - After fusion, all SNAREs are in the same membrane
  • Cis-SNARE complexes are inactive and must be disassembled by NSF and SNAP proteins
  • ATP hydrolysis by NSF provides energy for SNARE complex disassembly
  • After disassembly, SNAREs are recycled for another round of fusion

Section 4: COPI Vesicles

Coat Proteins

COPI vesicles utilize a distinct set of coat proteins:

  • Coatomer - Heptameric complex (α, β, γ, δ, ε, ζ-COP subunits)
  • α-COP and β'-COP - Form the outer layer of the coat
  • γ-COP, δ-COP, and ζ-COP - Adaptor subunits that recognize sorting signals

G-proteins

ARF1 (ADP-ribosylation factor 1) regulates COPI vesicle formation:

  • Activated by ARF-GEFs at Golgi membrane
  • GTP-bound ARF1 recruits coatomer complex
  • Inactivated by ARF-GAPs, triggering uncoating

Mechanism of Vesicle Budding

  1. ARF1-GDP is recruited to Golgi membrane and activated by ARF-GEF
  2. ARF1-GTP inserts into membrane and recruits coatomer
  3. Coatomer binds to cargo with KKXX or KDEL retrieval signals
  4. Coat assembly drives membrane curvature
  5. Vesicle pinches off and uncoats after ARF1-GTP hydrolysis

Vesicle Docking and Fusion Mechanism

  • Specific Rab proteins (Rab1, Rab2) guide COPI vesicles
  • Vesicles use v-SNAREs to recognize t-SNAREs on target membranes
  • SNARE-mediated fusion releases cargo into target compartment

Section 5: Clathrin-Coated Vesicles

Coat Proteins

Clathrin-coated vesicles feature a distinctive polyhedral lattice:

  • Clathrin - Triskelion-shaped protein forming outer cage
  • Adaptor Proteins (AP complexes) - Link clathrin to membrane and cargo (AP1, AP2, AP3, AP4)

G-proteins

ARF family proteins regulate clathrin-coated vesicle formation:

  • ARF1 activates AP1 at trans-Golgi network
  • ARF6 involved in plasma membrane endocytosis
  • Activation recruits adaptors and initiates coat assembly

Mechanism of Vesicle Budding

  1. ARF-GTP recruits adaptor proteins to membrane
  2. Adaptors bind cargo and recruit clathrin triskelia
  3. Clathrin polymerization forms curved lattice
  4. Accessory proteins (e.g., epsin) further bend membrane
  5. Dynamin GTPase pinches off vesicle
  6. Hsc70 mediate uncoating

Vesicle Docking and Fusion Mechanism

  • Rab5 guides early endosome fusion
  • Rab7 directs late endosome/lysosome fusion
  • v-SNAREs pair with t-SNAREs
  • SNARE complex formation drives membrane fusion
