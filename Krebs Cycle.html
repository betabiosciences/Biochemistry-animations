Mitochondrial Membrane Structure

Membrane Organization

Mitochondria possess a double membrane system that creates distinct compartments essential for cellular respiration:

  • Outer Mitochondrial Membrane (OMM): Smooth, permeable to molecules up to ~5 kDa
  • Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (IMM): Highly folded (cristae), impermeable to most ions and molecules

Membrane Permeability

Outer Membrane: Contains porins (VDAC) that allow passage of molecules up to 5000 Da, including ATP, ADP, Pi, pyruvate, and small metabolites.

Inner Membrane: Highly selective barrier with specific transporters. Impermeable to protons, creating the proton gradient essential for ATP synthesis.

Key Membrane Proteins

  • Pyruvate Carrier (MPC1/MPC2): Transports pyruvate into matrix
  • ATP Synthase (Complex V): Synthesizes ATP using proton gradient
  • Electron Transport Complexes (I-IV): Create proton gradient
  • Adenine Nucleotide Translocator (ANT): Exchanges ATP/ADP

Mitochondrial Compartments

  • Intermembrane Space: pH ~7.0, similar to cytosol composition
  • Mitochondrial Matrix: pH ~8.0, contains TCA cycle enzymes, ribosomes, mtDNA

Pyruvate Transport to Matrix

Transport Mechanism

Pyruvate transport from cytosol to mitochondrial matrix occurs via the Mitochondrial Pyruvate Carrier (MPC), a heterodimeric complex of MPC1 and MPC2 subunits.

Transport Properties

  • Stoichiometry: 1 pyruvate : 1 H+ (symport)
  • Driving Force: Proton gradient (ΔμH+)
  • Km: ~150 μM for pyruvate
  • Inhibitors: UK5099, α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamate
Pyruvate(cytosol) + H+(intermembrane) → Pyruvate(matrix) + H+(matrix)

Regulation of Transport

Allosteric Regulation:

  • Inhibited by acetyl-CoA (product inhibition)
  • Enhanced by CoA availability
  • pH-dependent activity (optimal at physiological pH)

Metabolic Control:

  • Coordinated with pyruvate dehydrogenase complex activity
  • Responds to energy charge of the cell
  • Influenced by Ca2+ levels

Acetyl CoA Synthesis

Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex (PDC)

The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex catalyzes the irreversible oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.

Complex Composition

  • E1 (Pyruvate dehydrogenase): Contains TPP, catalyzes decarboxylation
  • E2 (Dihydrolipoamide acetyltransferase): Contains lipoic acid, forms acetyl-CoA
  • E3 (Dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase): Contains FAD, regenerates oxidized lipoic acid
Pyruvate + CoA + NAD+ → Acetyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH + H+
ΔG°' = -33.5 kJ/mol

Reaction Mechanism

  1. Decarboxylation: E1-TPP forms hydroxyethyl-TPP intermediate
  2. Oxidation: Transfer to lipoic acid on E2, forming acetyl-lipoamide
  3. Acetyl Transfer: CoA attacks acetyl-lipoamide, forming acetyl-CoA
  4. Regeneration: E3-FAD reoxidizes dihydrolipoamide, reducing NAD+

Cofactors Required

  • Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) - E1
  • Lipoic acid - E2
  • Coenzyme A - substrate
  • FAD - E3
  • NAD+ - final electron acceptor

Substrate Channeling Mechanism

Concept of Channeling

Substrate channeling is the direct transfer of intermediates between active sites without release to the bulk solution, preventing loss of reactive intermediates.

Channeling in PDC

The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex demonstrates sophisticated channeling mechanisms:

  • Structural Basis: E2 core with radiating E1 and E3 subunits
  • Lipoyl Domains: Mobile arms that swing between active sites
  • Intermediate Protection: Reactive acetyl and reduced lipoyl groups remain protein-bound

Advantages of Channeling

  • Prevents side reactions of unstable intermediates
  • Increases local substrate concentration
  • Reduces product inhibition
  • Enhances overall reaction rate
  • Maintains stoichiometric coupling

Molecular Mechanism

The lipoyl domain acts as a "swinging arm" that:

  1. Visits E1 active site for acetyl group acceptance
  2. Swings to E2 core for acetyl-CoA formation
  3. Moves to E3 for reoxidation
  4. Returns to E1 to restart the cycle
Channeling Efficiency ≈ 90-95%
(Minimal intermediate release to solution)

Regulation of Acetyl CoA Synthesis

Covalent Modification

Phosphorylation/dephosphorylation is the primary regulatory mechanism:

  • PDK (Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Kinase): Phosphorylates and inactivates E1
  • PDP (Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Phosphatase): Dephosphorylates and activates E1

Allosteric Regulation

PDK Activation (↓ PDC activity):

  • Acetyl-CoA (product inhibition)
  • ATP (high energy state)
  • NADH (reduced NAD+/NADH ratio)
  • Fatty acids (metabolic preference)

PDK Inhibition (↑ PDC activity):

  • Pyruvate (substrate activation)
  • ADP (low energy state)
  • Ca2+ (signaling activation)
  • Mg2+ (cofactor availability)

Hormonal Regulation

  • Insulin: Activates PDP, promotes dephosphorylation
  • Glucagon/Epinephrine: Activates PDK via cAMP
  • Cortisol: Increases PDK expression
Active PDC ⇌ Inactive PDC-PO4
← PDP (Ca2+, Mg2+) | PDK (ATP, Acetyl-CoA) →

Metabolic Integration

Regulation ensures coordination with:

  • Glycolysis flux
  • Fatty acid oxidation (Randle cycle)
  • TCA cycle activity
  • Cellular energy state

The Krebs Cycle (TCA Cycle)

Cycle Overview

The tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle completely oxidizes acetyl-CoA to CO2, generating NADH, FADH2, and GTP.

Acetyl-CoA + 3NAD+ + FAD + GDP + Pi + 2H2O →
2CO2 + 3NADH + 3H+ + FADH2 + GTP + CoA

Key Enzymes and Properties

1. Citrate Synthase:

  • Catalyzes condensation of acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate → citrate
  • ΔG°' = -32.2 kJ/mol (irreversible)
  • Regulated by ATP (inhibitor) and ADP (activator)

2. Aconitase:

  • Citrate ⇌ isocitrate via cis-aconitate
  • Contains [4Fe-4S] cluster
  • Reversible, near equilibrium

3. Isocitrate Dehydrogenase:

  • Isocitrate + NAD+ → α-ketoglutarate + NADH + CO2
  • Rate-limiting step
  • Activated by Ca2+, ADP, inhibited by ATP, NADH

4. α-Ketoglutarate Dehydrogenase:

  • Similar to PDC mechanism
  • α-KG + CoA + NAD+ → succinyl-CoA + NADH + CO2
  • Inhibited by succinyl-CoA, GTP, NADH

5. Succinate Thiokinase:

  • Succinyl-CoA + GDP + Pi → succinate + GTP + CoA
  • Substrate-level phosphorylation
  • Only step generating high-energy phosphate directly

TCA Cycle Products & Regulation

Remaining Enzymes

6. Succinate Dehydrogenase (Complex II):

  • Succinate + FAD → fumarate + FADH2
  • Embedded in inner mitochondrial membrane
  • Part of electron transport chain
  • Inhibited by oxaloacetate, malonate

7. Fumarase:

  • Fumarate + H2O ⇌ malate
  • Reversible, stereospecific
  • Near equilibrium reaction

8. Malate Dehydrogenase:

  • Malate + NAD+ ⇌ oxaloacetate + NADH
  • Thermodynamically unfavorable (ΔG°' = +29.7 kJ/mol)
  • Driven by citrate synthase reaction

Overall Products per Acetyl-CoA

3 NADH → 7.5 ATP (via ETC)
1 FADH2 → 1.5 ATP (via ETC)
1 GTP → 1 ATP
Total: ~10 ATP per acetyl-CoA

Regulation Points

  • Citrate Synthase: Product inhibition by citrate
  • Isocitrate Dehydrogenase: Primary control point
  • α-Ketoglutarate Dehydrogenase: Similar to PDC regulation

Anaplerotic Reactions

Reactions that replenish cycle intermediates:

  • Pyruvate Carboxylase: Pyruvate + CO2 → oxaloacetate
  • PEP Carboxykinase: PEP + CO2 → oxaloacetate
  • Essential for gluconeogenesis and biosynthesis

C-13 Isotope Labeling Experiment

Experimental Design

Two separate experiments using acetyl-CoA labeled with 13C at different positions to trace carbon fate through the TCA cycle.

Experiment A: Methyl Carbon Labeled

*CH3-COSCoA (methyl carbon labeled with 13C)

Methyl-Labeled Results - Round 1

  • Citrate Formation: Label enters at methyl position of acetyl unit
  • Aconitase Action: Due to prochiral nature, creates asymmetric citrate
  • First CO2 (Isocitrate DH): 0% labeled - comes from original oxaloacetate
  • Second CO2 (α-KG DH): 100% labeled - derived from original acetyl methyl
  • Succinate: Contains unlabeled carbon from acetyl carbonyl

Methyl-Labeled Results - Rounds 2-3

  • Round 2: No labeled CO2 released (label was lost in Round 1)
  • Round 3: No labeled CO2 released
  • Key Finding: Methyl carbon is completely lost as CO2 in first round

Experiment B: Carbonyl Carbon Labeled

CH3-*COSCoA (carbonyl carbon labeled with 13C)

Carbonyl-Labeled Results - Round 1

  • Citrate Formation: Label enters at carbonyl position
  • First CO2 (Isocitrate DH): 0% labeled - from oxaloacetate
  • Second CO2 (α-KG DH): 0% labeled - from original acetyl methyl
  • Succinate Formation: Contains the labeled carbon from acetyl carbonyl
  • Label Retention: 100% remains in cycle intermediates

Carbonyl-Labeled Results - Round 2

  • Succinate Symmetry: Label becomes equally distributed (50:50)
  • First CO2: 50% labeled - statistical release from labeled oxaloacetate
  • Second CO2: 50% labeled - from the same pool
  • Remaining Label: 50% retention in cycle

Carbonyl-Labeled Results - Round 3

  • Continued Dilution: Further label scrambling
  • CO2 Release: ~25% of each CO2 labeled
  • Pattern: Geometric decrease continues (25% retention)

Key Experimental Insights

  • Methyl vs Carbonyl Fate: Dramatically different outcomes
  • Immediate vs Delayed Release: Methyl lost in Round 1, carbonyl persists
  • Aconitase Specificity: Demonstrates asymmetric handling of citrate
  • Succinate Symmetry: Creates label randomization in Round 2+
  • Biosynthetic Implications: Carbonyl carbon available for anabolic processes
Methyl Label: 100% → 0% → 0%
Carbonyl Label: 100% → 50% → 25%
(Complete loss vs geometric dilution)

Biological Significance

  • Carbon Economy: Different fates optimize metabolic efficiency
  • Anabolic Precursors: Persistent carbons available for biosynthesis
  • Cycle Validation: Confirms intermediates are true cycle participants
  • Enzyme Mechanism: Reveals stereochemical specificity of TCA enzymes
