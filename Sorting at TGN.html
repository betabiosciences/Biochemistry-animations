Sorting and Assembly from Trans-Golgi Network

Overall Features of Sorting and Assembly from Trans-Golgi Network

The Trans-Golgi Network (TGN) is a major sorting and distribution center within the secretory pathway of eukaryotic cells. It represents the final compartment of the Golgi apparatus where proteins and lipids are packaged into specific transport vesicles for delivery to their final destinations.

Key Functions:

  • Protein Sorting: The TGN sorts proteins into different vesicle types based on specific sorting signals and receptors.
  • Vesicle Formation: It mediates the formation of various transport vesicles including clathrin-coated vesicles, COPI vesicles, and others.
  • Post-translational Modifications: Final modifications such as phosphorylation, sulfation, and proteolytic processing occur in the TGN.
  • Lipid Sorting: The TGN also sorts lipids for delivery to different cellular membranes.

Vesicle Types Formed at the TGN:

  • Clathrin-coated vesicles for transport to endosomes and lysosomes
  • AP-1-coated vesicles for transport to endosomes
  • AP-3-coated vesicles for transport to lysosomes and related organelles
  • AP-4-coated vesicles for transport to endosomes and the plasma membrane
  • Secretory vesicles for constitutive and regulated secretion

Targeting to Lysosome: Mechanism and Associated Diseases

Lysosomal targeting from the TGN is a highly specific process that ensures hydrolytic enzymes reach the lysosome, where they degrade various biomolecules.

Synthesis of Mannose-6-Phosphate Tag:

Step-by-Step Process:

  1. Recognition in the cis-Golgi: Lysosomal hydrolases contain a specific signal patch (a three-dimensional structural feature) that is recognized by the enzyme N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphotransferase (GlcNAc-phosphotransferase).
  2. First Catalytic Step: GlcNAc-phosphotransferase transfers N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphate from UDP-GlcNAc to the 6-position of specific mannose residues on the high-mannose oligosaccharides of lysosomal enzymes, forming a phosphodiester intermediate.
  3. Formation of Blocked M6P Residue: This creates a covered M6P residue where the phosphate group is blocked by the attached N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc).
  4. Uncovering Enzyme Action: In the trans-Golgi network, the enzyme N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphodiester α-N-acetylglucosaminidase (commonly called the "uncovering enzyme") removes the GlcNAc cap.
  5. Exposure of M6P Signal: This uncovers the M6P residue, making it accessible for recognition by M6P receptors in the TGN.
  6. Quality Control: The M6P tag ensures that only lysosomal enzymes are efficiently targeted to lysosomes, while secretory proteins lacking this tag continue through the secretory pathway.

Key Enzymes:

  • GlcNAc-phosphotransferase: A hexameric enzyme (α₂β₂γ₂) that recognizes lysosomal enzymes and catalyzes the initial phosphorylation
  • Uncovering enzyme: Removes the GlcNAc cap to expose the M6P signal

Mechanism of Lysosomal Targeting:

  • Mannose-6-Phosphate (M6P) Tagging: In the cis-Golgi network, N-linked oligosaccharides on lysosomal enzymes are modified to contain mannose-6-phosphate residues.
  • M6P Receptor Binding: In the TGN, M6P receptors recognize and bind to the M6P tags on lysosomal enzymes.
  • Vesicle Formation: The receptor-enzyme complexes are packaged into clathrin-coated vesicles with the help of adaptor proteins (AP-1 and GGA).
  • Transport and Unloading: Vesicles deliver their cargo to late endosomes, where the acidic environment causes dissociation of enzymes from receptors.
  • Receptor Recycling: M6P receptors return to the TGN for another round of sorting.

Removal of Mannose-6-Phosphate Tag:

After lysosomal enzymes reach their destination, the M6P tag is removed to prevent re-binding to M6P receptors and ensure proper enzyme function within the lysosome.

Mechanism of M6P Removal:

  1. Lysosomal Delivery: M6P-tagged enzymes are delivered to late endosomes, which mature into lysosomes.
  2. Acidic Environment: The late endosome maintains an acidic pH (approximately ~5.0) which:
    • Causes dissociation of enzymes from M6P receptors
    • Activates acid phosphatases that remove the phosphate group
  3. Functional Consequences:
    • Prevents re-binding to M6P receptors, ensuring enzymes remain in the lysosome
    • May enhance enzymatic activity by removing potentially interfering modifications

Clinical Significance: Defects in M6P removal are rare but can contribute to lysosomal storage disorders by affecting enzyme stability or function within the lysosome.

I-cell Disease (Mucolipidosis II): Caused by deficiency in GlcNAc-phosphotransferase, the enzyme responsible for adding M6P tags to lysosomal enzymes. This results in mistargeting of multiple lysosomal enzymes to the extracellular space instead of lysosomes.

Targeting to Plasma Membrane

The TGN plays a crucial role in directing proteins to the plasma membrane through two main pathways: constitutive and regulated secretion.

Constitutive Secretion Pathway:

  • Default Pathway: Many membrane proteins and secreted proteins travel to the plasma membrane without specific sorting signals.
  • Continuous Process: Vesicles continuously bud from the TGN and fuse with the plasma membrane.
  • Role in Membrane Maintenance: Delivers lipids and proteins needed for membrane expansion and turnover.

Regulated Secretion Pathway:

  • Signal-dependent: Proteins are stored in secretory granules and released only upon specific signals (e.g., hormonal or neuronal stimulation).
  • Granule Biogenesis: Involves selective aggregation of secretory proteins in the TGN, followed by budding of immature secretory granules.
  • Examples: Hormone secretion (insulin), neurotransmitter release, digestive enzyme secretion.

Role of Granins in Regulated Secretion:

Chromogranins and secretogranins are acidic proteins that play crucial roles in the sorting and packaging of regulatory peptides in the TGN for regulated secretion.

Chromogranin A (CgA):

  • Functions:
    • Promotes granule biogenesis by aggregating with other granule components in the TGN
    • Helps sort soluble proteins into secretory granules through its aggregation properties
    • Acts as a calcium buffer in secretory granules

Chromogranin B (CgB) / Secretogranin I:

  • Functions:
    • Similar to CgA in promoting granule formation through aggregation

Secretogranin II (SgII) / Chromogranin C:

  • Functions:
    • Processed to generate secretoneurin, a neuroactive peptide
    • Contributes to the sorting of specific peptide hormones

Mechanism of Granin-Mediated Sorting:

  1. Aggregation in the TGN: Granins self-aggregate and co-aggregate with other granule proteins in the mildly acidic, high-calcium environment of the TGN
  2. Selective Concentration: This aggregation selectively concentrates regulated secretory proteins while excluding constitutive secretory proteins
  3. Membrane Budding: The aggregated core induces membrane curvature and budding of immature secretory granules
  4. Granule Maturation: During maturation, granins are processed to generate bioactive peptides
  5. Calcium Binding: Granins bind calcium, which helps stabilize the granule matrix and regulates processing
